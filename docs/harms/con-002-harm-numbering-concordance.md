# CON-002 · Harm Numbering Concordance

> **Authority.** A **record**, and permanent (ST-01 Rule 2: a concordance once published is never
> deleted). It answers one question: a citation of "H*n*" in a v2026.10.1-era document, which harm
> of the ML-2.3 register did it mean?

> **Disclosure first.** The corpus's own HX-000 v2026.10.1 was written by an agent who **had not
> matched its harm list to ML-2.3 §H.2.2**, the tier-1 text it claims to supply (DEF-040). Only
> **H1, H10, H11 and H15** correspond cleanly. "H*n*" and "HX-*nn*" are therefore **not**
> interchangeable for n ≥ 2, even though the numbers look alike.

## 2. Reading rule

A citation of "H*n*" in any file dated before v2027.1 is read through this table. A citation of
"HX-*nn*" means the ML-2.3 harm. The two forms are never treated as equivalent without this table.

## 3. What is kept

The v2026.10.1 file's estate findings ("FAILS" on H15–H25) remain **valid evidence about the
estate**, re-filed under the mapped harms. H18 and H19 have no home in the register, and that is
why UH-03 is recommended for admission ([HX-000 v2027.1 §15](hx-000-register-v2027-1.md)).

## 4. Falsifier

A row is wrong if the mechanism text of the v2026.10.1 entry describes a different causal chain from
the mapped harm's mechanism. "Partial" rows are the agent's judgement and should be checked first.
