# MX-005 — Scope and Boundaries

> **Authority.** `governing`, tier 3. Sets what the estate **is about** and, more
> importantly, what it is **not about**. Subordinate to
> [ML-2.3](../license/the-maher-license.md),
> [HX-000](../harms/hx-000-the-fourteen-structural-harms.md) (tier 1a), and the
> [Charter](mx-000-charter.md).

> **Disclosure first.** The most useful thing this instrument does is refuse. An
> estate that declines to state its boundaries will, over time, be read as
> claiming everything — and will then be judged against claims it never made and
> cannot meet. [Annex A](#annex-a--the-out-of-scope-register) is the operative
> part of this document; §§1–9 explain how it was built.

---

## 1. Position

Scope documents fail in one of two directions.

**Too narrow**, and the estate cannot explain why half its content exists. **Too
broad**, and scope ceases to exclude anything, which means it ceases to be scope.

The test this instrument uses: **a scope statement is only doing work if some
reasonable, attractive, requested thing falls outside it.** Annex A therefore
lists things the estate has actually been asked for, or would plausibly be asked
for, and declines them — not a list of absurdities nobody wanted.

## 2. Definition

The estate's scope is the intersection of **three** conditions. A subject is in
scope only if **all three** hold:

1. **It concerns the governance of a document estate, a licence, or the harms
   either may cause.** This is the subject condition.
2. **It can be discharged by publishing a checkable text.** This is the
   **capability** condition — the estate's sole capability is authorship.
3. **Its central claims can be stated with a falsifier** per
   [ST-04](../standards/st-04-falsifiability-and-claim-cards.md).

Condition 2 is doing most of the exclusionary work and is the honest one. The
estate cannot run a service, staff a process, employ anyone, verify anyone, or
measure anything about a reader. A subject requiring any of those is out of scope
**by incapacity**, not by disinterest, and the distinction is recorded per case in
Annex A.

### 2.1 Three kinds of out-of-scope

| Kind | Meaning | Reversible? |
|---|---|---|
| **Out by principle** | The estate refuses on stated grounds and would refuse with unlimited resources | **No** — reversal requires amending a governing instrument |
| **Out by incapacity** | The estate would do it and cannot | **Yes** — on acquiring the capacity |
| **Out by partition** | In scope for the estate, but belongs to another pillar | n/a — a filing answer, [MX-004](mx-004-terms-of-top-levels.md) |

Conflating the first two is the commonest dishonesty in scope documents. "We
don't do that" reads as a principle and is often a capacity confession. Annex A
labels every row.

### 2.2 The single-participant boundary

A blunt consequence of [DEF-031](../00-governance-map/defect-register.md): **the
estate has one participant**, so every subject requiring two people is out by
incapacity. That includes independent appeal review, independent harm review,
ratification, counsel review, and archive attestation — five functions its own
instruments specify ([REG-08 §3.2](../registries/reg-08-role-and-authority-registry.md)).

The estate's documents are therefore **in scope** and its **processes are not**.
Stated as a single sentence: *this estate can write a rule it cannot run.*

The same subject splits across the boundary depending on whether it is discharged
by writing or by doing.

## 4. Mechanism

### 4.1 The boundary test, in order

```
Is it about document governance, licensing, or harms?   no → out of scope (subject)
Can it be discharged by publishing a checkable text?    no → out by INCAPACITY
Can its central claim carry a falsifier?                no → out by PRINCIPLE
Does another pillar own it?                             yes → out by PARTITION (MX-004)
                                                               otherwise → IN SCOPE
```

The order matters. Testing capability before falsifiability means the estate does
not spend effort deciding whether an unfalsifiable subject is within its powers.

### 4.2 Recording a boundary decision

Every out-of-scope determination is recorded in Annex A with: the request, the
kind (principle / incapacity / partition), the instrument relied on, and — for
incapacity — **what would have to change**. That last column is the honesty
mechanism: an incapacity row with a blank "what would change" column is a
principle row wearing a disguise.

### 4.3 Boundary review

Incapacity rows are reviewed when a capacity changes. **The trigger is the
capacity, not the calendar** — if a second participant joins, five rows change the
same day, without waiting for the 12-month interval.

## 5. Worked instance

**A request that is attractive, reasonable, and refused: "publish a comparison of
this licence against the standard open-source licences."**

Nothing about this is unreasonable. It would help readers, it is squarely about
licensing, and the estate has thought about its own licence more than most.

Run §4.1:

| Gate | Result |
|---|---|
| Subject — governance / licensing / harms? | **Pass.** Plainly in subject |
| Capability — dischargeable by a checkable text? | **Pass.** It is a document |
| Falsifiability — can the central claim carry a falsifier? | **Here it fails** |
| Partition | Moot |

Why the falsifiability gate fails: a licence comparison's load-bearing claims are
claims about **legal effect** — that clause X of licence A is broader than clause
Y of licence B. Those claims are falsifiable in principle, by legal analysis. But
the estate has **no counsel** ([REG-08](../registries/reg-08-role-and-authority-registry.md)),
so it cannot state the evidence class of such a claim above **E0**, and a
comparison table of E0 legal claims presented in the confident register of a
comparison table is precisely the counterfeit
[ST-04 §3](../standards/st-04-falsifiability-and-claim-cards.md) names.

So the row lands as **out by incapacity**, and "what would have to change" reads
*counsel review*. Not out by principle — the estate wants this document and would
publish it tomorrow with a lawyer's involvement.

**The instructive contrast.** The estate *does* publish the
[enforceability two-track table](../license/enforceability-two-track-table.md),
which makes legal-effect claims about its **own** licence on the same E0 evidence.
Why is one publishable and the other not?

The honest answer is that the distinction is **thin**: self-assessment about one's
own instrument, clearly labelled, is a disclosure of the author's own reasoning;
self-assessment comparing a third party's instrument imports the same weak
evidence into a claim *about someone else*, where the label protects the estate
and not the party described. That is a real difference and it is not a large one.
A reader who thinks the two-track table should be withdrawn on the same reasoning
has a good argument, and it is filed as `open` under
[ST-06](../standards/st-06-objections-standard.md) rather than answered here.

## 6. Failure modes

| # | Failure | Mechanism | Current state |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | **Incapacity dressed as principle** | Refusing on high grounds what one merely cannot do | Guarded by Annex A's mandatory kind + remedy columns |
| F2 | **Scope creep by publication** | A document published outside scope, scope later widened to fit | **Live risk** — the estate has published faster than it has reasoned about boundaries |
| F3 | **Boundary never tested** | No requests received, so no refusals exercised | **Actual** — most Annex A rows are anticipated, not received |
| F4 | **Capacity change unnoticed** | Five rows should flip and do not | Trigger is §4.3; no watcher |
| F5 | **Asymmetric self-exemption** | Estate applies a standard to others it relaxes for itself | **Named in §5** and unresolved |
| F6 | **Partition confusion** | Out-by-partition read by a reader as out-of-scope entirely | Mitigated by [MX-004](mx-004-terms-of-top-levels.md) cross-refs |

## 7. Limits

1. **Most refusals are hypothetical.** The estate has received few requests. A
   boundary that has never been pushed is a prediction about future conduct.
2. **§5's distinction is thin** and the estate says so rather than defending it.
3. **One participant means one boundary-setter.** The person deciding what is out
   of scope is the person who would otherwise have to do the work — a conflict with
   no structural control ([REG-08 §7.1](../registries/reg-08-role-and-authority-registry.md)).
4. **No Arabic text**, while `prevailing_text: ar`.
5. **Condition 3 (falsifiability) is applied unevenly.** Several published
   instruments carry E0 central claims. The estate's application of its own gate
   to its own output is the subject of
   [RB-05](../rubrics/rb-05-harm-review-gate.md), which **returns the estate's own
   work as RETURNED**.

## 8. Falsifier

> **IF FALSE.** Falsified if:
>
> - **(a)** a published document in the estate fails §2's three conditions —
>   meaning the estate is operating outside its own stated scope;
> - **(b)** any Annex A row labelled *out by principle* is shown to be a capacity
>   limitation, or vice versa;
> - **(c)** an incapacity row has a blank "what would change" column;
> - **(d)** a capacity is acquired and the affected rows are not revised within 30
>   days.
>
> **(a) is the sharpest and the estate expects it to fire.** The
> [enforceability two-track table](../license/enforceability-two-track-table.md)
> is the leading candidate on condition 3, by the estate's own reasoning in §5.
> Publishing a falsifier one expects to fire is the alternative to narrowing the
> gate until nothing fails it.

## 9. Obligation

**On the estate:**
- Apply the three conditions of §2 **before** publishing, not after.
- Label every refusal as principle, incapacity, or partition — never leave the
  kind unstated (§4.2).
- Fill the "what would change" column for every incapacity row (§4.2).
- Revise on capacity change, not on the calendar (§4.3).
- Do not widen scope to retrospectively legitimise a published document (F2).
- Apply condition 3 to its own instruments as strictly as to a request it refuses
  (§7.5).

### Annex A.1 — Reading the register

Count: **11 out by principle, 8 by incapacity, 1 by partition.** The eight
incapacity rows collapse onto **four** missing things: a second person, counsel, a
runner, and Arabic authoring capacity. Three of the four are the same acquisition
problem, and one is a script.

That is the most compact honest statement of the estate's condition available
anywhere in the corpus.
