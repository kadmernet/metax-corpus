# MX-016 · Complaints, Appeals and Due Process

**What this instrument governs.** What a person may do when this estate decides something about
them, and what the estate owes them while deciding.

**The structural problem it must answer honestly.** Every appeal here is ultimately heard by the
same person who made the original decision, because the estate has one Curator. An appeals
instrument that pretended otherwise would be worse than none — so §4 states the conflict plainly
and specifies what can be done about it that does not require a second institution.

---

## 1. Position

The estate makes decisions **about people** in four places: assessment
([RB-01](../rubrics/rb-01-d1-d5-rubric.md)), credential revocation, academy chartering and
revocation ([MX-004](mx-004-terms-of-top-levels.md)), and moderation
([MX-008](mx-008-moderation-and-enforcement.md)). Each is a decision a person can be wronged by.

Published at `/credentials/review/appeals/`, and it is the instrument the credential ladder depends
on: a credential nobody can contest is an assertion by its issuer.

**The macro register.** Under **HX‑05** (asymmetry of the record) the characteristic injustice of
a modern platform is not the wrong decision but the **unappealable** one — an account closed by a
system whose reasoning is unavailable, to a party with no address to write to, and no record that
the dispute occurred. What makes that a structural harm rather than bad service is the asymmetry:
the institution holds the record, the account, the reasoning and the power to revise all three,
and the affected person holds a screenshot. **Due process is the cheapest available correction**,
and its cost is almost entirely in refusing convenience.

---

## 2. Definition — the five guarantees

These bind on every decision the estate makes about a person. They are not subject to scale,
volume or the Curator's discretion.

**1. Reasons in writing, before the appeal window opens.** A decision is communicated with its
reasoning, citing the instrument and section relied on. *"Your submission did not meet the
standard"* is not a reason; *"D3 scored 1: the dossier lists planned features rather than stating
what the harness cannot determine"* is.

**2. The text relied on, as it stood.** The person receives the rubric, standard or policy version
that governed them — retrievable under
[MX-013 Rule 4](mx-013-records-and-versioning.md). A decision under a text the person cannot now
read is unappealable in practice.

**3. A stated window, and a stated response time.** Thirty days to appeal; thirty days for the
estate to respond. Both published, both running from a dated notice.

**4. A record that the dispute happened.** Whether the appeal succeeds or fails. This is the
guarantee most often absent elsewhere and the one HX‑05 most directly concerns.

**5. No detriment for appealing.** An appeal does not affect standing, eligibility or any other
matter. An estate in which appealing is costly has an appeals process that exists on paper.

### 2.1 What an appeal may contest, and what it may not

| May contest | May not contest |
|---|---|
| The decision was made under the wrong text | That the text itself is wrong — that is a [correction](../00-governance-map/corrections-log.md) or an [open question](../00-governance-map/open-questions-and-ratifications.md) |
| A dimension was scored against evidence that does not support it | That the rubric should have different dimensions |
| Procedure was not followed | That the procedure should be different |
| An undeclared conflict of interest existed | That the reviewer is insufficiently senior |
| Material supplied was not considered | That the decision was harsh |

**The right-hand column is not a brush-off.** Each of those is a legitimate criticism with a
different route: the correction route for an error, the open-questions route for a decision, the
[objections standard](../standards/st-06-objections-standard.md) for a disagreement about a
standard's content. **An appeals process that also entertained standard-revision requests would
resolve neither**, and a person redirected must be told *which* route applies rather than simply
refused.

---

## 3. Mechanism — the three stages

**Stage 1 — Reconsideration.** The original decision-maker re-examines against the appeal. Cheap,
fast, and resolves the common case: a reviewer who missed supplied material. **Fourteen days.**

**Stage 2 — Fresh review.** A different reviewer, not involved in stage 1, reviews the original
submission **de novo** — not the appeal, and not the first reviewer's reasoning. Conflicts declared
and published. **Thirty days.**

**Stage 3 — Curator determination.** Final within the estate. Published as a determination under
[Parts D and G](../license/determinations-procedure.md), with reasons, in the
[Determinations Registry](../registries/reg-07-determinations-registry.md).

### 3.1 Stage 2 is the load-bearing stage and the one the estate cannot presently staff

A fresh review by an uninvolved reviewer is the only stage that can catch a **wrong judgement**
rather than a procedural slip. Stage 1 catches oversights; stage 3 is the same person who set the
rules.

**The estate has no reviewer pool.** No credential has been issued, no reviewer has been appointed,
and the ethics reviewer role remains `[VERIFY]`
([V-04](../00-governance-map/verification-log.md),
[RAT-04](../00-governance-map/open-questions-and-ratifications.md#rat-04--name-the-ethics-reviewer-and-the-successors)).
So stage 2 is currently **unstaffable**, and the honest consequence is that an appeal today would
collapse from three stages to two — with both remaining stages held by the same person.

This is recorded rather than concealed because it is the difference between a specified process and
an operating one, and the estate has enough instances of that confusion already.

---

## 4. The conflict at the centre, stated plainly

**Stage 3 is heard by the person who wrote the rubric, appointed the reviewer, and published the
standard.** There is no appellate body. There is no external ombudsman. The estate has one Curator.

Four things the estate does about it, none of which dissolves it:

**Publication.** Every determination is published with reasons, and a challenge to it is published
alongside it **whether or not it succeeds**
([enforceability table §4](../license/enforceability-two-track-table.md)). A biased decision is
therefore a biased decision *on the record*, which is a materially different thing from a private
one.

**§G.5, the humility clause.** A determination issued **in bad faith, to suppress dissent, or to
entrench the Steward's advantage is void** — and it runs *against* the Curator. It is one of the
provisions [GM-01 §5](../00-governance-map/precedence-and-conflict-rules.md) places outside
precedence entirely, so no instrument at any tier can weaken it.

**The correction obligation.** [§0.5.2](mx-000-charter.md#2-the-correction-obligation) removes from
the Curator the power to decline a valid correction, delete a published defect, or remove a
superseded claim. An appeal that establishes a factual error therefore forces a record he cannot
suppress.

**Succession.** [MX-015](mx-015-succession-and-continuity.md) exists so that the estate's
determinations survive the determiner — and the succession instrument is itself
[beyond his unilateral amendment](mx-000-charter.md#05-powers-placed-beyond-the-curator).

**What none of this provides.** Independence. A person who believes the Curator decided wrongly
about them has, within this estate, exhausted their options at stage 3. The estate's position is
that **saying so is better than implying otherwise**, and that the four mechanisms above make bad
faith expensive rather than impossible. A reader who finds that insufficient is not making a
mistake.

## 5. Worked instance — an appeal against a D3 score

A candidate is scored `pass with specified deficiency` on D3 and appeals, arguing the dossier's §5
did state limits and the reviewer read only §3.

**Stage 1.** The original reviewer re-reads §5. It distinguishes *planned features* from *what the
harness cannot determine* in its final paragraph — which the `Evidence` field shows was not
examined, because it records *"dossier §§1–4."* **D3 is re-scored 2. Determination amended.**
Fourteen days. Total cost: one re-read.

**Why it resolved at stage 1.** Because [RB-01 §3.1](../rubrics/rb-01-d1-d5-rubric.md) requires the
reviewer to record **what they actually examined.** Without that field the appeal would have been
the candidate's memory against the reviewer's, unresolvable, and would have escalated to a stage
the estate cannot currently staff.

**What is published.** The amended determination, the appeal, and the fact that the original review
examined §§1–4 of a five-section dossier. The last item is unflattering to the estate and is
published under guarantee 4, because a record that logs only successful decisions is not a record.

**The general lesson.** Most appeals are not disputes about judgement; they are disputes about
**what was looked at**. An assessment regime that records its evidence resolves them cheaply. One
that does not escalates them all to the stage where independence is most needed and least
available.

## 6. Failure modes

**Reasons that are conclusions.** *"Did not meet the standard."* Guarantee 1's test is whether the
person can identify what to change.

**The moving text.** A decision under a rubric that has since been amended, with the amended
version supplied. Guarantee 2 and [RB-01 §3](../rubrics/rb-01-d1-d5-rubric.md)'s freeze exist
against this.

**Stage 2 staffed by a colleague of stage 1.** In a small estate this is near-unavoidable, so the
conflict is **declared and published** rather than denied — the same treatment
[RB-05 §5](../rubrics/rb-05-harm-review-gate.md) applies to its own gate-holder.

**Detriment by implication.** No explicit penalty, but the appellant's next submission receives
closer attention. Undetectable and corrosive; the only protection is that reviewer assignment and
COI are published.

**The appeal that is a standards disagreement.** §2.1. Redirected, with the correct route named —
not refused.

**Silence.** The estate exceeds its own thirty days. Under guarantee 3 that is itself a breach and
belongs in the defect register, not in a queue.

---

## 7. Limits

**Class: structural, and it is decisive.** §4. One Curator, no appellate body, stage 3 heard by the
author of the rules.

**Class: practical.** **No appeal has ever been filed**, no determination has ever been issued, and
the [Determinations Registry](../registries/reg-07-determinations-registry.md) is empty. Stage 2 is
unstaffable (§3.1). Everything here is untested.

**Class: technical.** No mechanism enforces the windows. A missed thirty-day response is caught by
whoever notices.

**Class: jurisdictional.** This instrument creates no legal right and displaces none. Where
mandatory consumer law gives a person a better remedy, that law governs and this process does not
purport to substitute for it — [RAT-07](../00-governance-map/open-questions-and-ratifications.md#rat-07--jurisdiction-governing-law-and-the-liability-cap)
is open on jurisdiction and the liability cap, and `counsel_reviewed: false` applies here as
elsewhere.

---

## 8. Falsifier

1. **An appeal that cannot be routed by §2.1** — a genuine grievance that is neither an appeal, a
   correction, an objection nor an open question. Under-inclusive routing.
2. **Stage 2 never used**, either because it is unstaffable or because stage 1 resolves everything.
   **The likeliest**, and it would show the three-stage structure to be a two-stage one with a
   decorative middle.
3. **Appellants suffering detriment**, measurable only if enough appeals exist to compare outcomes.
4. **The four §4 mechanisms proving insufficient** — a determination widely regarded as bad faith
   that §G.5 does not void, because the only party who can apply §G.5 is its subject. **This is the
   falsifier the estate cannot answer**, and it is stated rather than argued away.

---

## 9. Obligation

**For the estate:** reasons in writing citing instrument and section; the governing text as it
stood; the windows honoured; the record published whether or not the appeal succeeds; no detriment.
**And appoint reviewers** — stage 2 is the stage that catches wrong judgements and it currently
does not exist ([RAT-04](../00-governance-map/open-questions-and-ratifications.md#rat-04--name-the-ethics-reviewer-and-the-successors)).

**For a reviewer:** record what you examined. §5 shows that field resolving an appeal at the
cheapest stage; without it the same appeal escalates to the stage with the least independence.

**For a person appealing:** you are entitled to the reasons, the governing text, a response inside
thirty days, and a published record. You are **not** entitled to a hearing outside this estate, and
§4 says so plainly rather than letting you discover it at stage 3.

**Under ML-2.3:** §H.05 (asymmetry of the record), §G.5 (the humility clause, running against the
Steward), §H.19 (authorship of record — every determination names a natural person), §H.20 (chain
of responsibility), §H.30 (non-participation), §E.8 (anti-capture — the estate may not charge for
an appeal).
