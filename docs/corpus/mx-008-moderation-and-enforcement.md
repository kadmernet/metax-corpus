# MX-008 · Content Governance, Moderation and Enforcement

**What this instrument governs.** What may be published on any MetaX property, who decides, and
what happens when something must come down.

**The structural fact it must state first.** This estate has **almost no user-generated content**
by design — no comments, no forums, no profiles, no feeds. Moderation here therefore means
something narrower and stranger than usual: it governs **submitted artefacts, academy-published
material, and correction filings**, and nothing else.

---

## 1. Position

Most moderation policy is written for volume: millions of posts, automated triage, appeals at
scale. This estate has the opposite problem. Its surfaces are few, its contributors are identified,
and its output is long-form — which makes **every enforcement decision individually consequential**
and none of them a statistical matter.

Published at `/academies/operating/moderation/`, because the primary enforcement relationship is
with a **chartered academy** rather than with an individual. Its siblings are
[MX-016](mx-016-appeals-and-due-process.md), which supplies due process for every decision here,
and [MX-014](mx-014-research-ethics-and-exclusions.md), which holds the research red lines.

**The macro register.** Under **HX‑06** (erosion of the deliberative commons) the familiar
platform failure is moderation **at a scale that forbids judgement** — rules applied by systems
that cannot read context, to people with no address to appeal to. The estate's response is not
better automation but a **smaller surface**: no feed to moderate, no comment section to police, no
recommendation system to tune. Refusing the surface is cheaper and more honest than governing it
well, and it is the reason this instrument is short on procedure and long on what does not exist.

---

## 2. Definition — the four governed surfaces

| Surface | What is published | Who decides |
|---|---|---|
| **Estate instruments** | This corpus | The Curator |
| **Submitted artefacts** | Credential submissions, dossiers | Reviewers under [RB-01](../rubrics/rb-01-d1-d5-rubric.md) |
| **Academy material** | Content on `{slug}.metax.academy` | The academy curator, subject to §3 |
| **Correction filings** | Public submissions to the registers | The Curator, with §4's publication duty |

### 2.1 What has no moderation surface, deliberately

**No comments.** Anywhere. **No forums.** **No user profiles** beyond a deterministic identity
mark ([MX-022](mx-022-identity-and-sessions.md)). **No feed, no recommendations, no trending, no
social graph.**

This is not an omission awaiting a roadmap. Each of those surfaces requires a moderation apparatus
the estate cannot staff, generates engagement dynamics
[MX-023](mx-023-attention-and-human-sovereignty.md) refuses, and creates a class of decision this
instrument would then have to make at volume. **The estate's moderation policy is substantially a
list of things it declined to build**, and that is the honest characterisation rather than a
limitation.

---

## 3. Mechanism — the red lines

Nine prohibitions bind every surface, including academy subdomains. They are **not waivable** by
any academy charter, and the first three are outside precedence entirely
([MX-000 §0.4](mx-000-charter.md#04-the-three-refusals)).

**The three refusals — no exception of any kind.** Laboratory instruction in biology. Core Riba.
The sexualisation of minors. **No research exception, no artistic exception, no contextual
reframing, no amendment toward weakness.**

**Six further red lines:**

| Line | Scope |
|---|---|
| Human-subject research | Excluded per [MX-014](mx-014-research-ethics-and-exclusions.md) and the BCIA human-cell exclusion |
| Operational instruction for harm | Technique whose primary use is injury, intrusion or deception |
| Undisclosed commercial content | Editorial presentation of paid material — [MX-009 §2.1](mx-009-advertising-and-commercial.md) |
| Fabricated evidence or credentials | Including a fabricated source, citation or superseded text |
| Personal data of third parties | Published without consent, in any submission or academy page |
| Claims banned under [ST-04](../standards/st-04-falsifiability-and-claim-cards.md) | Unfalsifiable claims presented as established; the banned-claim register at `/method/epistemics/banned-claims/` |

### 3.1 Why "operational instruction for harm" is narrow rather than broad

The estate teaches search optimisation, attention mechanics, network technique and security. Each
is dual-use, and a broadly drafted prohibition would forbid the curriculum.

**The line is drawn at *primary use*, not at *possible misuse*.** Teaching how a ranking system
works is permitted; teaching how to poison one is not. Teaching how an attention loop is built is
permitted — and [ST-03](../standards/st-03-limits-and-bridge.md) makes the ethical limit
**mandatory** in the same series — while teaching how to defeat a person's own attempt to disengage
is not.

**The estate's position is that understanding a mechanism is a precondition of resisting it**, and
that a curriculum forbidden from explaining how the machine-mediated internet works would produce
readers more dependent, not less. That position is contestable and is filed as such: the strongest
objection to it, together with the estate's reply, belongs at
`/license/falsification/objections/` under [ST-06](../standards/st-06-objections-standard.md).

## 4. Mechanism — enforcement, and what must be published

Four escalating actions. **Each is published**, and the publication is not discretionary.

| Action | Applies to | Published |
|---|---|---|
| **Correction required** | A specific claim or passage | The requirement and the outcome |
| **Withdrawal** | A page or submission | The withdrawal and the reason |
| **Charter suspension** | An academy | Suspension, grounds, and cure conditions |
| **Revocation** | An academy or credential | The determination, with reasons |

### 4.1 The publication duty is the instrument's core

**A removal that is not recorded did not happen.**

Enforcement action is published under
[MX-013](mx-013-records-and-versioning.md) in the appropriate register, and the record states what
was removed, on which ground, and by whom. **The removed text itself is retained** under
[Archive Register Class A or B](../00-governance-map/archive-register.md) — non-governing, banner
attached, retrievable.

**Why retention rather than deletion.** Under **HX‑25** (silence in the record) an institution
that removes material without trace becomes one whose past cannot be audited: a reader cannot
distinguish *never published* from *published and withdrawn*, and cannot assess whether the
estate's enforcement is consistent or convenient. The three refusals are the **only** exception —
material breaching them is deleted rather than archived, and the *fact and ground* of deletion are
still published.

**And rejected enforcement is published too.** A demand that something be removed, refused by the
estate, is published with reasons — the same rule that governs
[rejected corrections](mx-013-records-and-versioning.md). This is what stops the enforcement queue
becoming a quiet channel for pressure.

## 5. Worked instance — an academy publishes an unfalsifiable claim

A chartered academy publishes a page asserting that its method *"produces mastery in ninety days"*
with no falsifier, no evidence class and no date.

**Classification.** Not a red-line breach of the first three kinds. It is a
[ST-04](../standards/st-04-falsifiability-and-claim-cards.md) banned claim: an unfalsifiable
assertion presented as established.

**Action: correction required**, not withdrawal. The academy is given the specific defect — no
falsifier, no evidence class, no date — and the instrument relied on. Fourteen days.

**If corrected**, the correction and its outcome are published, and the matter closes. No sanction,
no record of wrongdoing beyond the fact that a correction was required and made.

**If not corrected**, escalation to withdrawal of the page, then charter suspension with stated cure
conditions. Each step published; the original text retained with a non-governing banner.

**The academy's route.** [MX-016](mx-016-appeals-and-due-process.md) — three stages, reasons in
writing, the governing text as it stood, thirty days, **no detriment for appealing.** Note the
honest limitation: stage 2 requires an uninvolved reviewer and the estate has no reviewer pool
([MX-016 §3.1](mx-016-appeals-and-due-process.md)), so an appeal today would collapse to two stages
both held by the Curator.

**What this instance shows.** The first action is a **correction, not a removal**, and the
escalation is slow, published and appealable at every step. An estate whose first response to an
unfalsifiable claim were removal would be enforcing a standard it has not yet taught — and the
banned-claim register exists precisely so that the standard is published before it is enforced.

## 6. Failure modes

**Enforcement by convenience.** Acting on what is embarrassing rather than on what breaches a red
line. The publication duty of §4.1 is the only real control: an inconsistent pattern becomes
visible once every action is on the record.

**Silent removal.** §4.1. The failure this instrument is most concerned with, and the easiest to
commit under pressure.

**Red-line creep.** "Operational instruction for harm" expanded until it covers the curriculum.
§3.1's *primary use* test is the boundary.

**Escalation skipping.** Suspension before a correction was ever required. The four actions are
ordered and the order is part of the due process.

**The moderation surface arriving by accident.** A "reader responses" feature, an academy comment
section, a submission gallery — each creating a moderation obligation the estate declined in §2.1
and cannot staff. **Any new user-content surface requires a policy amendment first**, not
afterwards.

---

## 7. Limits

**Class: practical.** **No enforcement action has ever been taken.** No academy is chartered, no
credential issued, no submission assessed, no correction filed by an external party. Every
procedure here is untested, and §5's worked instance is hypothetical.

**Class: structural.** Enforcement, appeal and rule-making are held by the same person
([MX-016 §4](mx-016-appeals-and-due-process.md)). The mitigations are publication, §G.5's humility
clause, and the correction obligation placed beyond the Curator — none of which supplies
independence.

**Class: technical.** No mechanism detects a red-line breach. The banned-claim check would live in
[SENTINEL](../standards/st-13-static-build-and-sentinel.md) and does not run
([DEF-001](../00-governance-map/defect-register.md#def-001)).

**Class: jurisdictional.** This instrument creates no legal right and displaces none. Where local
law compels removal, the estate complies and **publishes the fact and the ground** to the extent
the law permits — and where it does not permit even that, the estate records that a removal
occurred which it may not describe.

---

## 8. Falsifier

1. **A breach that fits no red line but plainly should be actionable** — showing §3
   under-inclusive.
2. **A red line catching material the estate would want to publish**, showing it over-broad.
   §3's "operational instruction for harm" is the candidate, and §3.1 is the narrowing already
   applied.
3. **An enforcement action taken without publication.** Falsifies §4.1 in practice and is the most
   serious available failure, because it would mean the record is not the record.
4. **The declined surfaces of §2.1 proving indispensable** — if the estate cannot function without
   a comment or discussion surface, the moderation policy becomes a real obligation rather than a
   list of refusals, and this instrument would need rewriting rather than amending.

---

## 9. Obligation

**For the estate:** publish every enforcement action, including the ones that reflect badly, and
every **refused** demand for removal. Retain the removed text under
[MX-013](mx-013-records-and-versioning.md) except where a refusal requires deletion — and publish
the fact of deletion even then. Escalate in order.

**For an academy curator:** §3's nine red lines are inherited by your charter and are not waivable.
Requirement 9 at `/academies/requirements/r9-red-lines/` states them at your charter, and
Requirement 11 obliges your own Part H Statement.

**For a reader:** if material on any MetaX property breaches §3, that is a valid filing at
`/library/record/corrections-log/`. **Refused filings are published with reasons**, so a rejection
is itself on the record and can be argued with.

**Under ML-2.3:** §H.06 (erosion of the deliberative commons), §H.25 (silence in the record —
§4.1's whole justification), §H.05 (asymmetry of the record), §H.19 (authorship of record), §H.30
(non-participation), §C.9 (Core Riba, one of the three refusals), §H.3(a) (the absolutes, which
bind an academy exactly as they bind the estate).
