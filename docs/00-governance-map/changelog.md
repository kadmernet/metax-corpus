# Changelog

Every release, with **the reason** — not merely the diff. A changelog that records what changed
without recording why lets a future reader see the decision but not the argument, which makes the
decision impossible to revisit properly.

Superseded text is retained permanently in [Archive Register](archive-register.md). Nothing is
deleted and nothing is silently edited
([Charter §0.8](../corpus/mx-000-charter.md#08-amendment)).

## v2026.10.1 — completion pass, 2026-09-16 · **the register is turned on its author**

The release's second production pass. Roughly **forty instruments** written: the registries family
complete (REG-01…08), the corpus family complete (MX-000…024), standards complete (ST-01…24),
agents complete (AG-000…012), BCIA written from nothing (BX-000…009), two rubrics, two lexicon
instruments — and **N-01**, the item that had survived every prior phase.

### N-01 — HX-000 re-versioned to twenty-five harms

[DEF-021](defect-register.md#def-021)'s second limb is discharged. §A.10 of
[ML-2.3](../license/the-maher-license.md) incorporated *HX-000 v2026.10.1* by reference into a
**Tier 1** instrument, and the document did not exist. It now does, at
[`hx-000-structural-harms.md`](../harms/hx-000-structural-harms.md).

**How it was done matters as much as that it was done.** The predecessor named fourteen harms,
sat at `v2026.09.4` under **ML-2.2** — a licence version that is
[permanently lost](../license/version-history.md) — and ended unfinished at an append-point.
Three acts, none an overwrite and none a deletion:

1. **create** the new path — the old one asserted *fourteen* in a corpus governing twenty-five,
   which [ST-15 §5](../standards/st-15-amendment-and-versioning-standard.md)'s clarified rule A1
   requires be corrected;
2. **append** the redirect, 301, at [SITE-03 §6.1](../site/routing-law.md);
3. **supersede** the old document in place, retaining its full text — ~89,000 bytes holding the
   mechanisms, evidence, counter-arguments and replies for H1–H11 that the operative register only
   summarises.

The identifier did not move. `HX-000` is immortal under
[ST-01 Rule 2](../standards/st-01-identifier-and-url-grammar.md) and belongs to the register, not
to either file. **A path is not an identifier.**

**Discharged provisionally, and the qualification is not a formality.** Nobody has compared the
new register against the licence's own enumeration.
[HX-000 §9(f)](../harms/hx-000-structural-harms.md) states this as its own falsifier and records
that the check *"should have been run before this document was published and was not."* Until it
is, §A.10 may still be dangling.

### What the re-version found, which was not the twenty-five harms

**Twelve of twenty-five are instantiated by this estate, and ten of the twelve are in the two
strata that did not previously exist.**

| Stratum | New? | Result |
|---|---|---|
| V Reflexive (H15–H19) | **Yes** | **All five FAIL** |
| VI Successor (H20–H25) | **Yes** | **Five of six FAIL** |
| I–IV (H1–H14) | No | 2 answered, 10 partial, 2 FAIL |

Filed as [DEF-033](defect-register.md#def-033). The reflexive five are: the register as
absolution (H15); disclosure as a trust amplifier (H16); self-assessment presented as assessment
(H17); incapacity absorbing capability (H18); volume as evidence (H19).

**The predecessor could not have found them.** A register of fourteen harms about computing and
networking had no category in which *"we disclose faster than we remedy"*, *"our honesty makes our
unchecked claims more persuasive"*, or *"we are prolific and unchecked"* could even be **stated**.
So the count did not rise from fourteen to twenty-five because eleven more harms were found in the
world. **It rose because the register was turned on its author, and eleven of the additions
landed.**

Under **H15**, publishing that tally is not a remedy. It is the move H15 names.

### DEF-034 — a stale register is a false instruction

[AG-008 §5.1](../agents/ag-008-agent-collaboration-protocol.md) found the release's other **S1**,
and it was compound — neither limb a defect alone:

- the [task ledger](task-ledger.md) had **no rows** for ~40 written instruments;
- [AG-005 §5.1](../agents/ag-005-agent-tool-use-limits.md) recorded **zero compliance** with
  archive-before-overwrite.

A successor reading the ledger would have concluded those forty were still owed, begun drafting
them, and hit an unarchived overwrite path. **The realistic loss was forty instruments.**
Discharged by [task ledger §5b](task-ledger.md) (D-57…D-96), written in this pass.
[MX-013](../corpus/mx-013-records-and-versioning.md) treats register maintenance as an
accountability duty owed to readers; this makes it an **operational safety duty owed to the
successor**, and the two have different urgencies.

### The method finding, which generalises past this estate

[AG-010 §5.1](../agents/ag-010-agent-error-and-correction.md) classified nine agent errors across
the session and found a stable distribution: **every prohibition held; every affirmative duty
failed.**

Nothing protected was touched, nothing deleted, nothing deployed, no continuity claimed, no
disclosure softened. And archive-before-overwrite — an affirmative duty — was missed **six times
out of six**. Redrafted as a prohibition (*an agent may not overwrite a file absent from archive
Class B*) it would sit in the category that held perfectly. Same rule, same effect, different
compliance profile. **Place guards as prohibitions wherever a choice exists.**

### Seven free remedies, none adopted

[BX-006](../bcia/bx-006-phase-failure-catalogue.md) consolidated twelve phase failures and found
**seven with remedies requiring no amendment, no ratification, no vacant role and no reader
data**. [BX-006 §5.1](../bcia/bx-006-phase-failure-catalogue.md) explains why they stayed
invisible: checklist completeness (an author who writes all eight
[ST-24](../standards/st-24-page-prose-standard.md) parts has discharged the standard, and what is
off the checklist is not neglected but **invisible**); a phase-blind quality rubric
([RB-03](../rubrics/rb-03-instrument-quality-rubric.md) measures only two of four phases); and
incapacity absorbing capability, found twice independently.

### The cheapest outstanding check in the corpus

An **L-1 source pass** ([AG-011 §3.1](../agents/ag-011-agent-review-and-audit.md)): ten claims,
not chosen by the agent, opened against their cited instruments. **Twenty minutes.** No role, no
authority, **no second participant** — and it is the only available detection for **AE-1
fabrication**, the one error class the agent cannot see in its own output.

It has never been run once, across ~80 instruments and thousands of cross-references. That is
**H19** stated as a task.

## v2026.10.1 — 2026-10-01 · **the estate learns what its subject is called, and what its own site is for**

A navigation specification arrived whose §0 is titled *"Four corrections that invalidate parts of
the previous spec."* Three of the four invalidate work published nine days earlier. The fourth
invalidates not a fact but the **authority** the estate had granted to a whole class of facts, and
it is the most consequential single sentence the estate has received.

### The four corrections, in order of depth

**1. The name is MacroLifeTech, not MacroLifeTach.** The estate misspelt its own third pillar —
in [R-42](reconciliation-ledger.md#r-42--the-third-pillar-is-macrolifetech-not-bcia), in
[C-05](corrections-log.md#c-05), in [DS-00 §2](../design/ds-00-as-built-token-layer.md), in the
[task ledger](task-ledger.md), and in a published anchor slug minted from the error. The
specification rules the old spelling defective *"in every prior specification,"* which includes
this corpus.

**Why this is recorded first despite being the smallest error.** It is the cheapest defect in the
corpus and the most instructive, because **every mechanism the estate is proud of is a consistency
mechanism**, and a name misspelt consistently everywhere passes all of them. Cross-source
reconciliation, front-matter validation, dangling-link detection and live-code verification test
*agreement*, not *reference*. Forty-six ledger entries propagated the error faithfully. To catch
it you must know the referent, and no document in a corpus is the referent — the proper name of a
thing is held only by the person who named it. Logged as [C-13](corrections-log.md#c-13) and
[DEF-023](defect-register.md#def-023). The defective anchor is **retained beside** the corrected
one, because deleting a published address to conceal a spelling error trades a cosmetic defect for
a broken citation.

**2. The live site is a demo.** This re-levels nine claims published in v2026.09.4. Every fact in
C-07, C-08 and DEF-017…019 remains true of the bytes that were read; what changes is the standing
those facts were granted. The specification draws the line precisely: *"What survives from the
live build is the token layer, the component vocabulary and the chrome mechanics — the design
system, not the content."*

So the re-levelling is a split, not a retraction. **Design-layer findings stand in full** — the
token layer (R-43), the nine ambient animations with no reduced-motion escape (R-44, B-16), the
sub-44px touch targets: these are defects *in the design system*, which the demo was built to
establish and which production inherits intact. **Content-layer and posture findings are demoted
to findings about a prototype** — the ad slots, the no-JavaScript render failure, the flat-file
route grammar, the copy and counts and `FLAGSHIPS` inventory. Not withdrawn: a defect observed in
a prototype scheduled to become production remains a defect. But each acquires a remediation
window a production defect would not have.

**One consequence widens the Curator's options for the first time in three releases.** Because the
site is a demo, there is a clean window to remove the ad slots *before* MetaX's own Part H
Statement is ever published — which would let it read **remedied** on HX‑01 and HX‑04 rather than
*unremedied*. [RAT-15](open-questions-and-ratifications.md#rat-15) therefore changes shape: no
longer *how do we confess this harm* but *do we commit it at all*. The obligation running the
other way is immediate and unconditional: every page must carry the demo provenance strip, and
`/about/universe/demo-notice/` must exist. An estate that knows it is a demo and does not say so
commits **HX‑15** on the exact pages a reader would consult to check whether it commits HX‑15.
Logged as [C-14](corrections-log.md#c-14), [DEF-025](defect-register.md#def-025),
[B-20](launch-blockers.md).

**3. Anchors become routes.** Every `a.html#b` becomes `/a/b/`. Directory routes with a trailing
slash, backed by `index.html`, no `.html` in any published URL, depth capped at three segments.
Group headings that existed only as `<h6>` labels inside a mega-panel now resolve to routes, and
*that* is what produces the estate's third tier and its 45 group hubs. One defect closes by
dissolution rather than repair: **DEF‑2026‑042** — the query-parameter detail route invisible
without JavaScript — ceases to have a subject, since `/toptech/s/{S##}-{slug}/` is now a real
pre-rendered file. It is the cheapest closure in the register's history and the estate records how
seldom that is available.

**4. Every page carries ≥1200 words of substantive prose** — and, more importantly, *"an agent
that pads a page to hit a count has produced a worse artifact than an agent that reported the page
as thin."* Six page classes are named as thin by nature and exempted, filed as
[DEF‑2026‑043](defect-register.md#def-2026-043). The justification is the estate's own licence:
padding a cookie policy to 1200 words *"is exactly the behaviour HX‑15 describes, and the estate
cannot publish a register of that harm while committing it on thirty pages."* This is the first
instance on record of the harms register operating as a **design constraint on the estate's own
specification** rather than as a claim about others, and it is the most encouraging development in
this release.

### The methodological finding, which is the real content of this release

v2026.09.4 recorded that reading the live code found nine errors that cross-source reconciliation
could not, and drew the lesson that documents flatter while artefacts do not. This release records
the limit of *that* method.

> Reading the artefact tells you what is there. It cannot tell you what the artefact is **for**,
> or what its subject is **called**.

Two of this release's corrections were unreachable by documents and by code alike: a proper name
(C-13) and the difference between a demo and a production system (C-14). Both required the
Curator. The [Corrections Log §Method](corrections-log.md#method-what-this-log-has-learned) now
carries the resulting taxonomy — three error-finding methods, three blind spots, each one's blind
spot another's specialty — and the uncomfortable implication is recorded rather than avoided:
**only two of the three can be automated.** The third requires a person who knows what the thing
is called and what it is for.

The practical consequence is a new required field. Every claim about a live artefact must now
record its **provenance of purpose** — `demo`, `staging` or `production` — alongside its date.
Nine claims in v2026.09.4 lacked it. None was false; all nine were unlabelled, which is the
subtler defect and the one no consistency check will ever raise. Amended into ST-05 and enforced
at the Reconciliation Ledger's door ([DEF-024](defect-register.md#def-024)).

### What the compliance layer revealed about the estate

**The estate commits three of its own twenty-five harms, and two decisions account for all
three.** HX‑01 (attention expropriation) and HX‑04 (the human as product) are the ad slots.

is rules without mechanisms** — and a corpus that responds by writing better rules is treating the
symptom.

**Two rows read `unexamined`, which is worse than `FAILS`.** HX‑21 (foreclosure of the successor's
options) and HX‑24 (irreversible commitment) have not been honestly assessed against this estate.
A named failure is arguable; an unexamined harm is a question nobody asked.

### What the second pass found, and it is the release's most serious finding

**Writing down what the estate requires of itself keeps revealing that it is not doing it.** Three

> These are not four problems. They are one problem seen four times: **the estate has one

**This outranks the ad slots in consequence**, and the release says so plainly. The ad slots are

### Five new open questions, and what they have in common

[RAT-19](open-questions-and-ratifications.md#rat-19) — is `Method` a ninth pillar, or an agent's
addition to be folded? The specifying agent disclosed it as its own: *"`Method` as a ninth pillar
is my addition, not yours."* Adopted provisionally because a route manifest is unusable without a

[RAT-20](open-questions-and-ratifications.md#rat-20) — when does the estate leave demo, and by what

[RAT-21](open-questions-and-ratifications.md#rat-21) — is ≈385,000 words written, reduced or

[RAT-22](open-questions-and-ratifications.md#rat-22) — does the Arabic mirror ship with the English

[RAT-23](open-questions-and-ratifications.md#rat-23) — **should the estate pay for introductions at
all?** ST-23 specifies what a compliant referral program would look like and does *not* argue that
there should be one. A defensible position holds that any payment for a recommendation compromises
it however disclosed. Four commercial figures are also the Curator's.

The first four are questions about **scale and its authorship**, and three of them are explicitly
decay-sensitive: RAT-19 is cheap today and expensive at a hundred written pages; RAT-21 is cheap
before dispatch and meaningless after; RAT-22 compounds page by page. The open-questions file had
previously treated its entries as stable pending a decision. These are not, and each now records
what it costs to answer late.

RAT-23 is different in kind and is the more uncomfortable filing. It exists because an agent was
asked to add a subsystem, found no source for it, designed one, and then had to record that a
commercial instrument now governs the estate **by default, because nobody supplied an
alternative.** That is not the same artefact as a reconciliation of supplied material, and it
should not wear the same `canonical` badge without comment.

### What was deliberately not done

**The `MacroLifeTach` anchor was not deleted.** It is retained beside the corrected one. A broken
citation is the worse of the two defects, and Charter §0.8 forbids the silent removal either way.

**`Method` was not adopted as a settled ninth pillar.** An agent may not enlarge the estate it
documents on its own authority — ML‑2.3 §H.19 and §H.25 both bear directly — so the nine are
provisional and the fold remains available at a stated cost.

**The Scale Gate's three thresholds were not filled with plausible numbers.** They determine other
parties' legal obligations. The gate runs on its five qualitative tests until the Curator publishes
them, and a licensee relying on the only test actually published has complied.

**ST-23's prior question was not answered.** The instrument specifies a compliant referral program
and declines to argue that one should exist.

**The estate's own Part H Statement was not issued — and not postponed indefinitely either.** Both
failures are named in the register the estate wrote: an all-clear return makes MetaX the first
specimen of HX‑15, and indefinite delay is the same harm by omission, since a duty deferred until
convenient is a duty the deployer controls. The commitment made in its place is dated: **within
thirty days of HX-000 v2026.10.1's publication, whatever the ad decision is by then, naming a
natural person.**

**HX‑21 and HX‑24 were left `unexamined` rather than given a plausible disposition.** Writing
"partial" for a harm nobody assessed would have been the cheapest sentence in the release and the
least honest.

**The nine re-levelled claims were not rewritten to look correct.** C-07, C-08 and DEF-017…019
keep their original text. What was added is the standing they should have carried. A register that
edits its earlier entries to appear consistent is performing the failure it was built to prevent.

**The route manifest did not commit to a page count or a schedule.** The census is a shape. The
default posture is staged publication, because it is the only option of four that never requires
publishing a page nobody wrote.

**No page was generated.** The estate now holds a route manifest, a page contract, a prose
standard and a nav geometry — the complete apparatus for producing 321 pages — and has produced
none of them. That is the correct order, and it is stated here so that the next release can be
measured against it.

## v2026.09.4 — 2026-09-02 · **the license that names its own harms, and the first look at the code**

Two documents arrived together and did opposite things. One raised the estate's governing
standard; the other established that the estate does not currently meet the standard it already
had. Publishing both in one release is the point.

### ML-2.3 supersedes the license layer

[ML-2.3](../license/ml-2-3.md) is published as the Tier 1 instrument, carrying a **Part H** that
names twenty-five structural harms and makes them operative.

**Why a new license version rather than an amendment to ML-2.1.** Part H is not an addition to
the license; it changes what kind of instrument it is. ML-2.1 conditioned permission on
*conduct* — do not deceive, do not oppress, do not originate riba. Part H conditions permission on
**structure**: on harm that no actor intends, that arises from how a system is built and financed,
and that is invisible to the party causing it because the metric certifying success is the
instrument of the injury. A licence cannot acquire that by amendment, because it changes the
answer to "what is this document for."

**The four repairs 2.3 makes**, each named to a defect rather than presented as an improvement:

| Defect | Repair | Why it mattered |
|---|---|---|
| ML-D-001 | §H.1-bis, four roles | Duties addressed "deployments," so Author, Integrator, Operator and Commissioner each pointed at the others |
| ML-D-002 | §H.2-bis, the Scale Gate | Duties written for a platform of a hundred million users, applied to a person publishing a static site, teach that the instrument may be ignored |
| ML-D-003 | §F.4, the condition/covenant split | "Absolute" without a stated consequence is a preference. Eight breaches now end the grant; everything else is a promise enforced as a promise |
| ML-D-004 | §H.16, §H.17 | There was no compliance artefact, and no way for compliance to **fail** |

**The fourth repair is the one worth reading twice.** §H.16.3 provides that a Part H Statement in
which nothing is marked partially remedied or unremedied is **presumptively non-compliant**, and
is evidence of HX-15 rather than of compliance. A compliance document that cannot fail is not a
compliance document — and this clause is what obliges the estate to publish its own failures
below.

### The register grew from fourteen harms to twenty-five

Two strata were added, and both are complaints about **this instrument** rather than about
technology:

- **Stratum V — Reflexive** (HX-15…HX-19): a register that mandates disclosure will produce
  disclosure-as-substitute, consent rituals, responsibility draining into the seams of the supply
  chain the register itself defines, a compliance moat for incumbents, and the export of one
  estate's norms as though they were arithmetic.
- **Stratum VI — Successor** (HX-20…HX-25): the characteristic subject of the next decade's harm
  is a party who cannot object at the time of the injury — a future person, a future holder, or a
  synthetic agent standing where a person stood.

Five absolutes became eight. §H.3(a)(vii), developmental substitution, is the only one drafted
**disjunctively** rather than cumulatively — deliberately over-inclusive, because over-inclusion
is the correct error to make where the subject is a child.

The harm identifiers were renamed `H{n}` → `HX-{nn}`, repairing the collision between harms
`H12–H14` and duties `§H.12–§H.14`. The estate's own proposed disambiguation convention is
withdrawn ([C-10](corrections-log.md#c-10)): a convention that must be remembered is a defect.

### The estate was checked against its own code for the first time

The as-built design review fetched `index.html`, `css/style.css`, `js/components.js` and
`js/site.js`. It produced **nine corrections**, and the reason it produced so many is recorded in
the [Corrections Log](corrections-log.md#method-what-this-log-has-learned) as that log's most
important finding:

> The estate's documents were **internally consistent and externally wrong.** Thirty-seven
> entries of careful cross-source reconciliation found one such error. Reading the code found
> nine in an afternoon.

The errors ran in one direction — the documents described a more ambitious, more principled, more
architecturally elaborate system than the one running. Invented subdomains, invented gradients, a
pillar that was actually a program, a static-rendering law nobody implemented, and an
anti-tracking claim with ad networks underneath it.

**What this release publishes about itself, rather than fixing quietly:**

| Finding | Where |
|---|---|
| Ad networks are live on the content plane, engaging HX-01 and HX-04 | [DEF-019](defect-register.md#def-019), [B-19](launch-blockers.md) |
| Nine infinite animations, no `prefers-reduced-motion` | [DEF-016](defect-register.md#def-016), [B-16](launch-blockers.md) |
| The site does not render without JavaScript, contrary to its own law | [DEF-018](defect-register.md#def-018) |
| Detail pages are invisible to non-JS crawlers — in a corpus built to be cited by machines | [DEF-017](defect-register.md#def-017) |
| DS-01…DS-06 describe a design system the code contradicts | [DEF-020](defect-register.md#def-020) |
| ML-2.3 cites an ML-2.2 that does not exist | [DEF-021](defect-register.md#def-021) |

**The estate's own Part H exposure is stated rather than deferred.** With ad networks live and no
Part H Statement published, MetaX is positioned to become the first specimen of **HX-15,
compliance theatre** — the harm its own licence introduced. That is
[RAT-15](open-questions-and-ratifications.md#rat-15--will-the-estate-publish-its-own-harms-as-unremedied),
and it is a Curator decision with exactly two honest outcomes: remove the networks, or publish
HX-01 and HX-04 as unremedied with review dates.

### New instruments

[Task Ledger](task-ledger.md) (GM-10) and [Corrections Log](corrections-log.md) (GM-07) — the
first because an earlier agent lost its task list mid-way and produced a partial corpus with no
record of what remained, which is [HX-23](../license/ml-2-3.md#h28-custody-and-horizon) acting on
this estate's own work; the second because it was referenced by three instruments and had never
existed.

### Five new open questions, and why the list got longer

[RAT-14](open-questions-and-ratifications.md#rat-14--does-ml-22-exist) through RAT-18. Four of the
five are questions about **the estate's own honesty** rather than about external facts: does a
superseded version exist, will we publish our own failures, is our religious reasoning sound, and
does our site do what we say it does.

A governance corpus whose open-question list grows as it matures is behaving correctly. A list
that shortens every quarter would mean the questions were being closed by preference.

### What was deliberately not done

- **ML-2.2 was not drafted retrospectively.** ML-2.3 describes itself as repairing six defects in
  a document the estate has never held. Manufacturing a plausible 2.2 whose defects conveniently
  matched 2.3's repairs would be the estate fabricating its own history inside the instrument that
  defines *Ṣidq* as *"do not build the machine that makes the distinction between lying and telling
  the truth unrecoverable."* The gap is declared on the instrument's face instead.
- **DS-01 was not rewritten to agree with the code.** Its wrong sections are marked non-governing
  and retained, per Charter §0.8. A corpus that edits its errors into agreement cannot be audited
  for how long it was wrong.
- **The *Ḥifẓ al-ʿAql* extension was not asserted as settled.** It is published as contested
  ([RAT-16](open-questions-and-ratifications.md#rat-16--the-hifz-al-aql-extension)), and ML-2.3
  §0.4 makes the duties stand on secular evidence independently, so the obligation survives the
  analogy's failure.

## v2026.09.2 — 2026-08-30 · **the consolidation and the plane partition**

### The substantive change: the Static Principle is partitioned

`/about/static/` claimed *"no database, no login wall, no client-side fetch."* Authentication,
payment and per-user dashboards made that false.

**What was done, and why this way.** The claim was neither left standing nor quietly deleted. It
is **narrowed, dated, and published as a narrowing**, with the old sentence retained as superseded
text on the doctrine page itself in a new section, *Where the principle bends*. The estate now runs
two planes with a hard boundary — the content plane may be *decorated* by the identity plane but
never *depends* on it — and the boundary is **mechanically tested** rather than asserted.

The reasoning is in the source document's own words: *"a false claim on a doctrine page is exactly
the failure mode this universe exists to prevent."* Quietly contradicting the page would have been
easier and would have been the one failure the estate cannot survive.
