# Routing Law and the Redirect Record

**What this instrument is.** The law of address formation for the `metax.academy` estate, and the
permanent record of every address the estate has ever published. The
[Route Manifest](route-manifest.md) enumerates *which* routes exist; this document states *how an
address may be formed at all*, and what happens to one when it is retired.

**Why it is separate from the manifest.** The manifest changes whenever a page is added — it is
`semi-durable` and reviewed every six months. This document should almost never change: it is
`durable`, and a change to it invalidates every address in the estate simultaneously. Keeping the
law separate from the inventory means the law can be cited stably while the inventory grows.

---

## 1. Position

Routing sits at the boundary between the estate's design layer and its governance layer, and it is
usually mistaken for the former. The [Page Contract](page-contract.md) governs what a page
contains; [DS-00](../design/ds-00-as-built-token-layer.md) governs how it looks;
[ST-24](../standards/st-24-page-prose-standard.md) governs what it says. None of them can make a
claim *retrievable*, and retrievability is prior to all three: a page nobody can reach twice is a
page that was never published in any sense the estate cares about.

The parent hub for this instrument in the published estate is `/library/stack/`, and its
governance parent is [MX-000 §0.8](../corpus/mx-000-charter.md) — the no-silent-edits rule, of
which the redirect record at §6 is the address-space application.

## 2. Definition — what counts as a well-formed address

An address is well-formed if and only if all seven conditions hold.

1. **It is a directory path with a trailing slash**, backed by an `index.html`. `/method/rails/`
   is well-formed; `/method/rails` redirects to it; `/method/rails.html` is not an address in this
   estate at all.
2. **It contains no `.html`.** The two exceptions are the plain-text licence mirror
   `/license/ml-2-3/ml-2-3.txt` and the legacy redirect *sources* in §6, which name defunct
   addresses in order to retire them.
3. **It is at most three segments deep**, excepting the parametric families of
   [manifest §6](route-manifest.md#6-parametric-families).
4. **Its first segment is one of the nine pillar keys**, or `ar`, or one of the three utility
   routes.
5. **Every segment is lower-case, hyphen-separated, ASCII.** No underscores, no camel case, no
   percent-encoding, no trailing hyphens. Arabic content lives at `/ar/…` with **English slugs** —
   the mirror translates the prose, not the address.
6. **It appears in the Route Manifest**, or is generated by a declared parametric family with its
   generation gate satisfied.
7. **It is unique**, including case. Rule matching is case-sensitive throughout the platform, so
   `/Method/` is not an alias of `/method/` — it is a 404.

### 2.1 What would not count, and why each exclusion earns its place

`/toptech/branch-a/flagship-search.html` — carries an extension, so its successor cannot be a
directory without a redirect. Extensions bind an address to an implementation.

`/toptech/s/S01-seo-mastery/` — the series slug must be lower-case: `s01-seo-mastery`. Mixed case
in URLs produces two addresses for one page on case-sensitive infrastructure and one address on
case-insensitive infrastructure, so the same corpus behaves differently depending on where it is
hosted. That is a portability defect masquerading as a style choice.

`/ar/المنهج/` — Arabic prose, English slug. Percent-encoded non-ASCII paths are legal and
technically fine, and the estate declines them anyway: they are unreadable in a citation, break
when copied through systems that normalise differently, and make the two language mirrors
structurally incomparable. Parity is easier to *audit* when the two trees have identical shapes.

`/method/rails/falsifiability/examples/worked/` — four segments. See §3.3.

## 3. Mechanism

### 3.1 Resolution order

A request resolves in five steps, and the order matters because two of the steps can mask
failures in the others.

1. **Exact match** against a generated `index.html`. Serve.
2. **Trailing-slash normalisation.** `/a/b` → 301 → `/a/b/`.
3. **`_redirects` lookup.** Legacy flat files, retired routes, aliases. 301.
4. **Language prefix.** `/ar/…` resolves against the mirror tree; `hreflang` pairs are emitted on
   both sides.
5. **404.** Serves `/404`, which renders the nav and a link to `/search/`.

Step 5 is where a routing bug becomes visible, and step 3 is where it becomes invisible. A
generous redirect table can mask a broken manifest indefinitely: the page resolves, the reader is
satisfied, and nobody notices that the address they used is not the address the estate publishes.
The audit rule that follows from this is in §7's falsifier — **every `_redirects` entry must be
justifiable by a retirement recorded in §6.** An entry with no recorded retirement is either a
typo being papered over or a route that was renamed without a changelog line.

### 3.2 Root-absolute paths, and why this is a *law*

Every asset, script and internal link in every generated file must be root-absolute:
`/css/style.css`, `/js/site.js`, `/img/…`, `/toptech/start/diagnostic/`.

The source calls relative paths *"the single most common way this migration breaks,"* and the
mechanism is worth spelling out because it explains why the rule is absolute rather than advisory.
A relative reference is resolved against the *current directory*, which under directory routing is
the route's own depth:

| Page | `href="css/style.css"` resolves to | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `/` | `/css/style.css` | Works |
| `/toptech/` | `/toptech/css/style.css` | **404** |
| `/toptech/start/` | `/toptech/start/css/style.css` | **404** |
| `/toptech/start/diagnostic/` | `/toptech/start/diagnostic/css/style.css` | **404** |

The defect is therefore **depth-correlated and invisible at depth 0**, which is the depth of the
root hub — the page most often open while working, and the page a developer checks first. It does
not fail in the build. It does not fail in CI. It fails for the reader, page by page, with an
unstyled document and no console error for a missing stylesheet path that simply does not exist.
And because the estate also aspires to render without JavaScript, the degraded state is a page
with no header, no footer and no navigation.

Filed as [DEF-026](../00-governance-map/defect-register.md#def-026) before a single depth‑1 page
existed, and recorded at
[R-49](../00-governance-map/reconciliation-ledger.md#r-49--root-absolute-paths-are-a-build-law).
A defect that cannot be noticed by the person who causes it belongs in the register in advance.

### 3.3 The depth cap

Three segments, and the cap is an epistemological commitment rather than a technical one.

Each tier corresponds to a level of generality the estate claims to have: a pillar makes a claim
about a domain, a group hub about a method or family, a leaf about one object. A fourth tier
would require a level of generality between "family of methods" and "one object" — and in the 266
leaves enumerated in the manifest, no such level appears. Where a leaf seems to need children, one
of two things is true: it is a group hub misfiled as a leaf, or its children are its sections.
Both are resolved by re-scoping, never by adding depth.

The parametric exception at `/toptech/s/{S##}-{slug}/c{NN}/` is the single case where the
relationship is genuinely part-whole: a course is a constituent of a series in a way that no leaf
is a constituent of another leaf.

### 3.4 `navKeyFor()` and the one-line mirror

```js
function navKeyFor() {
  var seg = location.pathname.replace(/^\/+/, "").split("/");
  if (seg[0] === "ar") seg.shift();                 // Arabic mirror
  var k = seg[0] || "home";
  var KEYS = ["toptech","method","ascent","metax","academies",
              "credentials","library","license","about"];
  return KEYS.indexOf(k) > -1 ? k : "home";
}
```

The `shift()` is the mechanism by which the Arabic mirror requires **no separate navigation code
path**, and that is a parity guarantee rather than a convenience. A duplicated nav implementation
is how two language trees drift: one is fixed, the other is forgotten, and the divergence surfaces
as a reader complaint in the language nobody tests.

The `KEYS` whitelist is the second deliberate choice. An unrecognised first segment returns
`home` rather than an empty active state, which is the correct behaviour for `/search/`, `/404`
and any future utility route. A nav with nothing highlighted tells the reader they are nowhere.

## 4. Worked instance — retiring `/bcia.html`

The estate's live demo publishes `bcia.html`, a flat file. Under this law it must become
`/metax/bcia/`, and the full transition is five recorded steps.

**Step 1 — establish the correct address.** BCIA is not a pillar; it is one of nine programs under
MacroLifeTech (Meta‑X)
([R-42](../00-governance-map/reconciliation-ledger.md#r-42--the-third-pillar-is-macrolifetech-not-bcia)).
So the address is not `/bcia/`, which would re-encode the mis-levelling that R-42 corrected, and
it is not `bcia.metax.academy`, a hostname the estate does not have. It is `/metax/bcia/`, a group
hub inside the `metax` pillar.

**Step 2 — generate the tree.** `/metax/bcia/` as a group hub with its ten leaves, plus
`/metax/bcia/status/` as a *second* group hub with five leaves of its own — BCIA's honesty layer
at the same tier as its content.

**Step 3 — record the retirement.** `/bcia.html → /metax/bcia/ 301` and
`/bcia-status.html → /metax/bcia/status/ 301` enter the §6 record. Both are permanent. A citation
made in 2026 against `bcia.html` resolves in 2031.

**Step 4 — repoint internal links.** Every reference in `data.js`, the search index and the nav
becomes root-absolute and points at the new address. The old address survives *only* in the
redirect record.

**Step 5 — carry the demo strip.** The strip may never be suppressed anywhere under
`/metax/bcia/`, because a gated research program is precisely where a reader most needs to know
what is real ([Page Contract §5](page-contract.md)).

What this instance demonstrates is that a routing migration is not a rename. Step 1 was a
*governance* determination, step 3 a *permanence* obligation, and step 5 an *honesty* obligation.
Only steps 2 and 4 were technical.

## 5. Failure modes

**The relative path at depth.** §3.2. Depth-correlated, invisible at depth 0, silent in the
console. Signature: a page that looks correct on the root hub and unstyled everywhere else.

**The generous redirect masking a broken manifest.** §3.1. Signature: a `_redirects` file that
grows faster than the retirement record, and internal links that resolve via 301 rather than
directly. An internal link should never need a redirect — if it does, the link is stale and the
redirect is hiding it.

**Case drift.** `/Method/` linked from one page and `/method/` from another. On case-sensitive
infrastructure one is a 404; on case-insensitive infrastructure both work and the canonical tag
disagrees with the address the reader is on. Signature: analytics showing traffic to an address
absent from the manifest.

**Trailing-slash inconsistency in *emitted* links.** Step 2 of §3.1 will fix it for the reader
and will also double the request count for every internal navigation, and — more damagingly — a
link emitted without the slash will be *cited* without the slash, which reintroduces the two-forms
problem the canonical rule exists to eliminate.

**Deleting a redirect to tidy the file.** The most serious failure in this document, and the one
most likely to be committed by someone acting carefully. See §6.

**Percent-encoded slugs creeping into the Arabic tree.** Signature: `/ar/` paths that do not
structurally match their English twins, making parity unauditable.

## 6. The redirect record — an append-only instrument

**The rule.** `_redirects` is **append-only**. An entry may be added; an entry may never be
removed or edited. There is no exception, and the reasoning is the same reasoning that governs the
[Corrections Log](../00-governance-map/corrections-log.md) and
[MX-000 §0.8](../corpus/mx-000-charter.md).

An address the estate has published is a commitment it has made. Somebody has cited it, bookmarked
it, or linked it from a document the estate will never see. Removing the redirect does not tidy
the file — it **breaks that citation**, silently, at an unknown future moment, for a reader who
will conclude the claim was withdrawn rather than moved. That is a harm to the record (HX‑12) and
to the successor (HX‑20…HX‑25), committed for the sake of a shorter configuration file.

**The record.** Twenty-three entries at v2026.10.1, enumerated in
[manifest §1.5](route-manifest.md#15-legacy-redirects). Each is 301 — permanent, not 302.

**The one sanctioned survival of a defect.** `/macrolifetach.html → /metax/ 301` preserves a
misspelling that [C-13](../00-governance-map/corrections-log.md#c-13) rules a defect everywhere
else in the estate. It must survive here, because the purpose of the entry is to *retire* the
defective address, and an entry that does not name the defective address cannot retire it. This is
the only occurrence of *MacroLifeTach* permitted anywhere in the corpus or the build, and the
acceptance checklist's line 20 is drafted to except it.

**Growth is expected and is not a defect.** A long redirect table is evidence that the estate has
kept faith with its own past addresses. A short one, in an estate that has migrated its entire
route grammar, would be evidence of the opposite.

### 6.1 Appended 2026‑09‑16 — the HX‑000 re-version

| # | From | To | Code |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24 | `/harms/hx-000-the-fourteen-structural-harms/` | `/harms/` *(HX‑000 v2026.10.1)* | 301 |

**Twenty-four entries** at this amendment.

**Why this one is unusual, and why it is not a rename.** The predecessor document is **not
withdrawn**. It is retained in place, marked superseded on its face, and holds the full argument
for H1–H11 — mechanisms, evidence, strongest counter-arguments, replies, ethical mappings — which
the operative register only summarises ([HX-000 §8.2](../harms/hx-000-structural-harms.md)). So
the redirect serves a reader who arrives at the **old address expecting the governing register**,
while the old **document** remains reachable and citable at its own path.

This is the pattern [ST-15 §5](../standards/st-15-amendment-and-versioning-standard.md)'s
clarified rule A1 requires and it is worth naming, because it was not obvious. A1 forbids
**version-suffixed paths** and requires amendment at the permanent path; it does **not** forbid
**path correction** where a path states a fact that has become false. `…the-fourteen-structural-harms`
asserts *fourteen* in a corpus governing **twenty-five**. The reconciliation is three acts, none
of them an overwrite and none a deletion:

1. **create** the new path (`/harms/hx-000-structural-harms/`);
2. **record** this redirect, append-only;
3. **supersede** the old document in place, retaining its text.

[AG-005 §4.3](../agents/ag-005-agent-tool-use-limits.md) reaches the same three acts from the
tool side, and notes that the predecessor sits on the **protected-artefact list** — so overwriting
it was never available, which is the guard working rather than a choice being made well.

**The identifier did not move.** `HX-000` is immortal under
[ST-01 Rule 2](../standards/st-01-identifier-and-url-grammar.md) and belongs to the register, not
to either file. A path is not an identifier.

## 7. Falsifier

This law is wrong if any of the following is observed:

1. A published address satisfying all seven §2 conditions that nonetheless fails to resolve.
2. A relative asset path at depth ≥1 that resolves correctly — which would falsify §3.2's
   mechanism rather than confirm it.
3. A `_redirects` entry with no corresponding retirement recorded in §6 — showing either an
   undocumented rename or a typo being masked.
4. A leaf page that genuinely requires a fourth segment, where neither re-scoping to a group hub
   nor folding the children into sections produces a coherent page — which would falsify the depth
   cap of §3.3.
5. An `/ar/` address whose path does not structurally match its English twin, which would make
   §2's parity claim unauditable and is itself a filed defect under
   [manifest §1.6](route-manifest.md#16-arabic-mirror).

Items 2 and 4 are the interesting ones, because they would falsify *reasoning* rather than reveal
a mistake. Items 1, 3 and 5 are checkable by a build step and should be.

---

## 8. Obligation

**For the build agent:** emit root-absolute paths without exception (§3.2). Emit internal links
with trailing slashes so that no internal navigation passes through a 301. Emit `_redirects` from
the §6 record only, never by hand.

**For the editor:** an address is retired by *adding* a line, never by deleting a page. Renaming a
route is a retirement plus a creation, and both belong in the changelog. Adding a route means
adding it to the [Route Manifest](route-manifest.md) first, at the tier §3.3 permits.

**For the reviewer:** audit §6 against the changelog quarterly. The check is cheap and it is the
only one that catches an undocumented rename, which is the class of change that breaks citations
without breaking anything visible.

**Under ML‑2.3:** §H.26 (corpus hygiene) and §H.27 (reversibility) both bear on this instrument
directly, and §H.28 (custody and horizon) is the reason the redirect record is append-only rather
than merely long-lived. The estate asserts that its addresses outlive its infrastructure; that
assertion is only meaningful if retirement is recorded rather than performed.
