# LEX-01 · The Keyword Register

**What this instrument is.** Every term this estate uses in a defined sense, resolving to the
**single document that defines it**.

**The rule it enforces.** A term defined in two places is defined nowhere — the two definitions
drift at the first amendment, and the drift is invisible until someone exploits it. The register
names **one** defining instrument per term, and every other use links to it.

**Designated** as a Work of Foundational Character ([DL-1 row 5](../license/designation-list.md)).

---

## 1. Position

This corpus runs on roughly a hundred defined terms — *falsifier*, *designated*, *provenance of
purpose*, *plane*, *island*, *Core Riba*, *stratum*, *thin by nature*. Each carries a precise
meaning, and a reader who assumes the ordinary sense will misread the instrument.

Published at `/library/lexicon/entries/` with the method at `/library/lexicon/method/`, the roots
index at `/library/lexicon/roots/`, and contested terms at `/library/lexicon/contested/`.

**The macro register.** Under **HX‑02** (epistemic dependency), a body of knowledge whose
vocabulary is undefined requires a reader to defer: they cannot check a claim whose terms they
cannot pin down. Worse, undefined vocabulary **drifts silently** — a term's meaning shifts across
releases, earlier text acquires a sense its author did not intend, and nobody can point to when it
changed. A register with one defining instrument per term is the cheapest structural refusal, and
its cost is the discipline of not redefining a term where it is convenient.

---

## 2. Definition — what a keyword is

A term is a **keyword** if all three hold:

**1. It carries a defined sense** that differs from, or is narrower than, ordinary usage.

**2. It appears in more than one instrument.** A term used once is defined in place.

**3. Misreading it changes what an instrument requires.** This is the operative test — not
importance, but **consequence of error.**

### 2.1 What is not a keyword

**Identifiers.** `MX-024`, `HX-15`, `DEF-031` are governed by
[ST-01](../standards/st-01-identifier-and-url-grammar.md).

**Proper names.** `MacroLifeTech`, `BCIA`, `Meta‑X`. They name things rather than carry senses —
though *MacroLifeTech* has a register entry recording the corrected spelling, since the misspelling
is published in a redirect and a stale anchor ([C-13](../00-governance-map/corrections-log.md#c-13)).

**Ordinary technical vocabulary.** *Cache*, *schema*, *gradient*. Defined by the field.

The single-source rule: **one defining instrument per term.** Every other occurrence links to it
and does not restate it.

### 3.1 The four pairs a reader will most likely confuse

**Evidence class / decay class.** What supports a claim versus how fast it goes stale.

**Prevailing text / editorial standing.** Which governs on conflict versus what a divergence
*means* — a filed defect.

**Condition / covenant.** The estate's most consequential pair: Part H's twenty-five harms are
**described**, not prohibited, and nineteen of its duties are curable within 30/90 days.

**Designated / important.** Designation makes a Work **more demanding to adopt**, not better.

## 4. Worked instance — "falsifier"

Used in nearly every instrument. Defined **once**, at
[ST-04 §2](../standards/st-04-falsifiability-and-claim-cards.md).

**The definition carries four required properties** — observable, specific, adverse, available in
principle — and **four named counterfeits**: the tautological, the unreachable, the restated
caveat, and the one nobody would check.

**Why one definition matters here.** Without the register, "falsifier" would plausibly be
re-defined in ST-24 (where a Falsifier section is mandatory), in HX-001 (where each harm carries
one), and in RB-01 (where a candidate's claims need them). Three definitions, three drifts, and
within two releases a "falsifier" in a harm entry would mean something different from one on a
page.

**What the register requires instead.** ST-24, HX-001 and RB-01 each **link** to ST-04 §2 and
apply it. Where a family needs a variant — HX-001's harms use the same four properties with a
stratum-specific reading — the variant is stated **as a variant**, citing the parent.

**The estate's own live case.** ST-04 §2.1 records that the estate has written at least one of the
four counterfeits itself, and [HX-001 §7](../harms/hx-001-harm-audit-rubric.md) names a
deliberately unfalsifiable falsifier as defeating that rubric's purpose. **A single definition is
what makes that self-criticism checkable** — there is one standard to have fallen short of.

## 5. Failure modes

**Redefinition in place.** An instrument restating a term "for convenience." Drifts at the first
amendment.

**Ordinary-sense reading.** A reader taking *designated* as praise, *record* as importance,
*stratum* as prohibition. §3's third column exists for this.

**The undefined term that behaves like a keyword.** A term meeting §2's three tests and absent from
the register. §7's first falsifier, and **expected**.

**Arabic coinage without a register entry.** Under
[ST-11 §4](../standards/st-11-arabic-authoring.md) a coined Arabic term enters the Lexicon **with
its reasoning**; omitting the reasoning makes the coinage unchallengeable.

**The register drifting from its defining instruments.** A definition amended in ST-04 and the
register's summary column left stale — the register becoming a second source of truth for terms it
exists to single-source.

---

## 6. Limits

**Class: completeness.** Twenty terms. The corpus plausibly has a hundred, and the estate cannot
enumerate them without the sweep that
[ST-13](../standards/st-13-static-build-and-sentinel.md) would run
([DEF-001](../00-governance-map/defect-register.md#def-001)). **This is a starting register, not a
complete one**, and it says so.

**Class: linguistic.** **No Arabic entries exist.** The Lexicon is
[MX-020 limb 1](../corpus/mx-020-language-authority.md) material — **Arabic-authoritative** — which
means the register is currently missing its *governing* half, not merely a translation. That is a
sharper gap than elsewhere in the corpus.

**Class: technical.** Nothing enforces single-sourcing. An author may redefine a term and nothing
detects it.

---

## 7. Falsifier

1. **A term meeting §2's three tests and absent from §3** — expected, and every instance is a valid
   correction.
2. **A term genuinely needing two definitions** in different families, falsifying single-sourcing
   as universal.
3. **§3's third column proving wrong** — if the "error it prevents" is not the error readers
   actually make, the register is defending against imagined confusions.
4. **The Arabic half never arriving**, which would make a designated Arabic-authoritative
   instrument an English-only document. **Closest to firing**, and it falsifies
   [MX-020 limb 1](../corpus/mx-020-language-authority.md) rather than this register.

---

## 8. Obligation

**For any author:** before defining a term, check §3. If it is there, **link** — do not restate. If
it meets §2's tests and is absent, add the row in the same edit.

**For the estate:** run the terminology sweep; publish the Arabic entries, since the Lexicon is
Arabic-authoritative and currently has no authoritative text; and keep §3's summary column short
enough that it cannot drift into being a second definition.

**For a reader:** where a term in this corpus seems to carry more weight than its ordinary sense,
it probably does. §3 resolves it, and §3.1 lists the four pairs most often confused.

**Under ML-2.3:** §H.02 (epistemic dependency), §H.22 (language parity), §H.12 (harm to the
record), §H.26 (corpus hygiene), §A (definitions — where the licence's own terms govern).
