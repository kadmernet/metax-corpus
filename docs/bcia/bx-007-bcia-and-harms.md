# BX-007 — BCIA and the Structural Harms

> **This page will not resolve:** whether any harm has occurred. It maps *where in
> a reader's encounter* harms could occur; it does not assess whether they have
> ([§7.1](#7-limits)).

> **Authority.** `companion`. It **explains a relationship** between two
> instruments and construes neither.
> [ML-2.3](../license/the-maher-license.md) (tier 1) and
> [HX-000](../harms/hx-000-the-fourteen-structural-harms.md) (tier 1a) govern; this
> page sits far below both.

> **Disclosure first.** [HX-000](../harms/hx-000-the-fourteen-structural-harms.md)
> is **an unfinished draft** — complete through H11 of 25, titled for fourteen
> harms, ending at an append-point, and linking to a licence version that does not
> exist ([AG-012 §3.1](../agents/ag-012-agent-sunset-and-withdrawal.md)). This page
> maps BCIA against a document that is half-written, and the mapping is incomplete
> for exactly that reason.

## 1. Position

[ML-2.3](../license/the-maher-license.md) governs **twenty-five structural harms
in six strata**, incorporating [HX-000](../harms/hx-000-the-fourteen-structural-harms.md)
at tier 1a by §A.10. [BCIA](bx-000-bcia-charter.md) describes four phases of a
reader's encounter.

The connection is not decorative. Several harms in the licence are **phase-located**
— they can only occur at a particular point in a reader's encounter — and the
estate has never mapped which.

That matters practically. If a harm occurs at phase B, no amount of careful
drafting inside the page prevents it; the remedy lives in the arrival, and the
corpus's arrival machinery is the part
[BX-006 §3.1](bx-006-phase-failure-catalogue.md) identifies as weakest.

### 1.1 What this page does and does not claim

**Does:** locate harm classes in phases, so remedies are looked for in the right
place.

**Does not:** assess whether any harm has occurred, construe any licence
provision, or evaluate the eight absolutes.

### 1.2 The absolutes are named and never paraphrased

[ST-09 §3.3](../standards/st-09-summary-and-abstract-standard.md) prohibits
paraphrasing an absolute, because a paraphrase creates a second, non-identical
absolute. This page therefore **refers to** absolutes by their position in
[§H.3(a)](../license/the-maher-license.md) and quotes nothing.

One structural fact is repeated because it is load-bearing and is not a
paraphrase: **absolute (vii), developmental substitution, is disjunctive** — it
operates on its own — while the other seven are cumulative.

## 2. The phase-harm map

Mapping the six strata of [ML-2.3](../license/the-maher-license.md) onto BCIA
phases. **Stratum-level only** — individual harms are not construed (§1.2).

| Stratum | Subject | Phase(s) where instantiation is possible |
|---|---|---|
| **I — Cognitive** (HX-01…03) | Attention, comprehension, judgement | **C, I** — occurs during reading and integration |
| **II — Relational** (HX-04…06) | Trust, dependency, isolation | **A** — occurs after, in reliance |
| **III — Economic** (HX-07, 08, 09, 13) | Extraction, lock-in, asymmetry | **A** |
| **IV — Material** (HX-10, 11, 12, 14) | Physical and resource effects | **A** |
| **V — Reflexive** (HX-15…19) | Effects on the estate itself | All four |
| **VI — Successor** (HX-20…25) | Effects on those who come after | **A**, at long range |

### 2.1 Stratum I is the phase C/I stratum

Cognitive harms are the only ones that occur **while the reader is present**. All
others are downstream.

This gives the eight-part recipe a role it was not designed for: everything in
[ST-24](../standards/st-24-page-prose-standard.md) that resists fluency —
adverse Limits, runnable Falsifiers, worked instances ending in blockage — is a
**stratum I control**, applied at the only moment stratum I harms can occur.

And [BX-002 §3.1](bx-002-contact-phase.md) found the countervailing problem:
the corpus's form **raises** perceived clarity, and its disclosures function as a
trust amplifier with no control. So the estate's stratum I posture is
**contested at phase C** — good controls and a structural counter-pressure, with
no measurement of which dominates.

### 2.2 Most harms are phase A, where the corpus is weakest

Four of six strata instantiate at phase A — after the reader leaves.

[BX-004](bx-004-after-phase.md) established that phase A is the corpus's
**least-present** phase: everything it does there is passive, and the one active
protection (next review date in the stamp) arrived by accident from reasoning about
the estate's death
([BX-004 §2.1](bx-004-after-phase.md)).

So the majority of the licence's harm surface sits in the phase with the least
corpus presence. That is the central finding of this page and it is not comfortable.

## 3. Absolute (vii) is a phase A absolute

Developmental substitution — [§H.3(a)](../license/the-maher-license.md) absolute
(vii), the **disjunctive** one — concerns a reader's own development being
**replaced** rather than supported.

Locate it: substitution cannot be assessed at Contact. A reader reading is not yet
substituting. It is assessable only **after**, in how the material is used —
whether it stood in for the reader's own work.

Two consequences follow, and neither is a construction of the absolute:

1. **The absolute operates in the phase the corpus can least observe.** The estate
   cannot see reliance at all ([REG-06](../registries/reg-06-dataset-registry.md)).
2. **It is the only disjunctive absolute**, so it bites without the others — and it
   bites in the phase the estate is blindest to.

### 3.1 What the corpus does about it

| Mechanism | Phase | Serves (vii)? |
|---|---|---|
| [RB-04](../rubrics/rb-04-reader-comprehension-rubric.md) self-assessment | A | **Yes** — a reader tests their own comprehension, unobserved |
| [BK-01 thesis](../registries/reg-03-book-registry.md) | — | States the premise; **E0** |
| Worked instances a reader must re-derive | I, A | Partly |
| Obligation sections | A | Partly |

[RB-04](../rubrics/rb-04-reader-comprehension-rubric.md) is the estate's principal
answer to (vii), and it is **handed to the reader and never seen by the estate** —
which is the only design available given the refusals, and which means the answer's
effectiveness is permanently **E0**
([RB-04 §6.1](../rubrics/rb-04-reader-comprehension-rubric.md)).

## 4. Mechanism — using the map

```
a harm concern arises
  → which stratum?
  → which phase does §2 locate it in?
  → is the corpus present in that phase?
       C or I  → the eight-part recipe applies; see BX-002, BX-003
       A       → the corpus is largely absent; see BX-004 and expect no control
  → is a remedy in BX-006's free seven?
```

The map's use is **diagnostic**, identical in kind to
[BX-005](bx-005-phase-audit-method.md): it says where to look, not what to do.

## 5. Worked instance

**Locating one harm-shaped failure the corpus already recorded.**

[BX-004 §5](bx-004-after-phase.md) traced a reader who relied on
[MX-005 §2](../corpus/mx-005-scope-and-boundaries.md)'s capability condition, which
[MX-018 §6](../corpus/mx-018-third-party-dependencies.md) later found to be a
**DC-3 overclaim** on a governing instrument. The reader cannot discover this.

Run §4:

| Step | Result |
|---|---|
| Which stratum? | **II relational** — the reader's trust in a governing instrument was misplaced and is not correctable |
| Which phase? | **A** — the harm exists entirely in reliance |
| Corpus present? | **Barely** — passive mechanisms only |
| Free remedy available? | **Yes — PF-11** ([BX-006 §5](bx-006-phase-failure-catalogue.md)): the instrument does not link the defect filed against it |

**Terminal state: `result` — a stratum II exposure with a free remedy, unadopted.**

### 5.1 The finding

The estate's harms framework
([HX-001](../harms/hx-001-harm-audit-rubric.md),
[HX-002](../harms/hx-002-harm-instrument-map.md)) maps each harm to an
**answering instrument**. [HX-002](../harms/hx-002-harm-instrument-map.md) records
the estate's own state against each: 3 `FAILS`, 2 `unexamined`, 3 `answered`, 17
`partial`.

That mapping asks *which instrument answers this harm*. It does **not** ask *in
which phase could this harm occur, and is the corpus present there*.

The difference is not academic. An instrument can exist, be well-written, and
answer a harm **in the wrong phase** — which is exactly the §5 case:
[MX-013](../corpus/mx-013-records-and-versioning.md) and the
[corrections log](../00-governance-map/corrections-log.md) answer the
record-keeping harm at the moment of *filing*, and the reader is harmed at the
moment of *reliance*, and no instrument connects the two.

Filed per [ST-17 W6](../standards/st-17-worked-instance-standard.md):

> **Finding BX-007/1.** [HX-002](../harms/hx-002-harm-instrument-map.md)'s
> instrument map should carry a **phase column**. An instrument answering a harm in
> a phase where the corpus is absent is not an answer. Adding one column would
> reclassify an unknown number of the 17 `partial` rows, and the estate's four-of-six
> strata at phase A (§2.2) suggests the number is not small. Filed as a
> ratification question; not made here
> ([BX-000 §1.1](bx-000-bcia-charter.md): BCIA creates no duties).

## 7. Limits

1. **No harm assessment is made or implied** (opening line, §1.1).
2. **[HX-000](../harms/hx-000-the-fourteen-structural-harms.md) is a draft.**
   Stratum-level mapping is the most this page can honestly do; per-harm mapping
   awaits the re-version.
3. **No legal competence** (gap 3). Nothing here bears on
   [§H.3(a)](../license/the-maher-license.md)'s operation.
4. **The phase locations in §2 are asserted** from the strata's subject matter, not
   derived from the harms' texts — which for H12–H25 do not yet exist.
5. **§2.1's contest is unmeasured** and unmeasurable here
   ([REG-06](../registries/reg-06-dataset-registry.md)).
6. **No Arabic text**, while `prevailing_text: ar`.

## 8. Falsifier

> **IF FALSE.** Falsified if:
>
> - **(a)** a stratum is shown to instantiate in a phase §2 does not list;
> - **(b)** absolute (vii) is shown to be assessable at Contact — which would
>   relocate the estate's hardest absolute into a phase it can serve (§3);
> - **(c)** [HX-002](../harms/hx-002-harm-instrument-map.md) is shown to already
>   account for phase — **checkable in minutes** and would defeat §5.1;
> - **(d)** an instrument is shown to answer a harm in the phase where the harm
>   occurs, in a case §5.1 claims it does not;
> - **(e)** any absolute is paraphrased on this page (§1.2).
>
> **(c) is the cheapest and most consequential.** If HX-002 already carries phase
> reasoning, this page's finding collapses. The estate's reading is that it maps
> instruments to harms and never asks when.
