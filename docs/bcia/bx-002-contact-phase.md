## 1. Position

Phase C is the reader **actually reading**. It begins at commitment and ends when
they stop, and it is the phase the corpus is engineered for: eight parts in order
([ST-24](../standards/st-24-page-prose-standard.md)), twenty acceptance criteria
([SITE-02](../site/page-contract.md)), a register matched to a declared tier
([ST-16](../standards/st-16-audience-tier-standard.md)).

Precisely because the machinery is good, phase C has one failure mode that the
machinery **makes worse**:

> **Fluency.** A well-formed page produces the feeling of understanding at a rate
> uncorrelated with actual understanding — and the better the form, the wider the
> gap can grow without either party noticing.

[RB-04 §1.1](../rubrics/rb-04-reader-comprehension-rubric.md) states this as the
distinction between recognition and comprehension. BX-002's contribution is to
locate it in a phase and name what the corpus does about it.

## 2. What happens in contact

| Sub-phase | Reader activity | Corpus obligation |
|---|---|---|
| **C1 Orientation** | Locating the argument's shape | Position, Definition |
| **C2 Acquisition** | Taking in the mechanism | Mechanism, Worked instance |
| **C3 Stress** | Finding where it breaks | Failure modes, Limits |
| **C4 Closure** | Knowing what is owed | Falsifier, Obligation |

The eight parts map cleanly onto four sub-phases, two parts each. That is not
coincidence — [ST-24](../standards/st-24-page-prose-standard.md)'s recipe was built
for reading, and it is the corpus's strongest piece of design.

### 2.1 The variation rule and why it exists

[ST-24](../standards/st-24-page-prose-standard.md) requires all eight parts and
permits **varying which section leads**. A corpus of eighty pages in identical
order produces a specific phase C failure: the reader **stops reading and starts
scanning**, because position predicts content.

Scanning is not reading. A scanning reader reliably reaches C1 and C4 — the shape
and the obligation — and skips **C3 entirely**, because Failure modes and Limits
sit in the middle where scanning is weakest.

So the variation rule is not stylistic. It is a defence of sub-phase C3, which is
where the reader learns what the page cannot do.

## 3. The fluency problem

### 3.1 Why this corpus is unusually exposed

| Property | Effect on fluency |
|---|---|
| Consistent eight-part form | **Raises** perceived clarity; predictable structure reads as mastery |
| Heavy cross-referencing | **Raises** apparent authority; every claim appears sourced |
| Tabulated mechanisms | **Raises** apparent precision; tables read as data |
| Self-critical disclosures | **Raises** apparent honesty, and thereby trust in unchecked claims |

The fourth is the uncomfortable one. A page that opens by admitting a failure earns
credibility that transfers to everything after it — including claims nobody has
verified. **Disclosure is doing persuasive work it was not intended to do.**

[MX-017 §5](../corpus/mx-017-conflicts-of-interest.md) treats disclosure as a
substitute for independence. BX-002 records the side effect: in phase C, disclosure
functions as a **trust amplifier**, and the estate has no control against it.

### 3.2 The defences that exist

| Defence | Mechanism | Effective? |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence classes | [ST-07](../standards/st-07-evidence-and-verify.md) E0–E4 labels | **Partly** — labels are present and easily skimmed past |
| Falsifiers | [ST-04](../standards/st-04-falsifiability-and-claim-cards.md) | **Partly** — most are never run |
| Limits sections | [ST-03](../standards/st-03-limits-and-bridge.md) | **Partly** — C3 is the scanned-past zone (§2.1) |
| Worked instances that fail | [ST-17](../standards/st-17-worked-instance-standard.md) W2/W4 | **Strongest** — a blockage cannot be read as success |

The fourth defence is the only one that resists scanning, because a trace ending in
`blockage` or `indeterminate` **cannot be skimmed into a positive impression**. A
reader who takes nothing else from a page takes away that the mechanism stopped.

That is a stronger argument for [ST-17](../standards/st-17-worked-instance-standard.md)
than ST-17 makes for itself: it justifies the worked instance as a defect-finding
tool for the author, and its larger value may be as the **only scan-resistant
honesty signal** for the reader.

## 4. Mechanism — auditing a page at phase C

```
C1: can a reader state the argument's shape after the first two sections?
C2: does the worked instance TRACE, or merely illustrate?   (ST-17 §2.1)
C3: are Failure modes and Limits adverse, or decorative?
C4: is the obligation actionable without re-reading?
```

C3 is the discriminating question. A Limits section listing only the limits that
flatter — *"this is new"*, *"more work is needed"* — is decorative. An adverse one
names what the page got wrong.

## 5. Worked instance

**Testing the scan-resistance claim on one page.**

Take [AG-005 agent tool use limits](../agents/ag-005-agent-tool-use-limits.md).
Simulate a scanning reader: read the disclosure block, the tables, the headings,
and the obligation — skipping prose.

| What a scanner takes away | Accurate? |
|---|---|
| The agent has write access to everything | **Yes** — disclosure block |
| There is a protected-artefact list | **Yes** — §3.1 table |
| Delete and deploy are refused | **Yes** — §3 table |
| Tools are constrained | **Yes** |
| **"The controls are working"** | **NO** |

The scanner's impression is **wrong in exactly one place**, and it is the important
one. [AG-005 §5.1](../agents/ag-005-agent-tool-use-limits.md) records that
**every overwrite this session skipped the archive step** — zero compliance on the
one affirmative duty — and that finding lives in **prose**, in a sub-section of the
worked instance, not in any table.

Now test the §3.2 claim. Does the worked instance's terminal state survive
scanning? [AG-005 §5](../agents/ag-005-agent-tool-use-limits.md) ends its step
table with a row reading *"**Step 2 not performed**"* and a bolded terminal state
*"`result` — one systematic breach."*

**It does survive.** A scanner reading only tables and bold text hits *"one
systematic breach"*.

**Terminal state: `result` — the claim holds on this page, narrowly, and only
because the breach was tabulated.**

### 5.1 The finding

Scan-resistance is **not a property of the worked instance**. It is a property of
**the terminal state being in a table or in bold**.

[ST-17 W3](../standards/st-17-worked-instance-standard.md) requires step
tabulation and [W4](../standards/st-17-worked-instance-standard.md) requires the
terminal state to be named honestly — but **neither requires the terminal state to
be tabulated or emphasised**. An author who traces a blockage in unbroken prose
complies fully with ST-17 and produces a page whose central adverse finding is
invisible to a scanning reader.

Several pages in this corpus do exactly that.

Filed per [ST-17 W6](../standards/st-17-worked-instance-standard.md):

> **Finding BX-002/1.** [ST-17](../standards/st-17-worked-instance-standard.md)
> should require the **terminal state to be visually prominent** — tabulated, bolded,
> or on its own line — not merely stated. Scan-resistance is the worked instance's
> most valuable property at phase C and is currently produced by accident. This is
> an amendment to a tier 5 standard and is filed as a ratification question, not
> made here ([BX-000 §1.1](bx-000-bcia-charter.md): BCIA creates no duties).

## 7. Limits

1. **Whether any reader has understood anything here is unknown and
   unmeasurable** (opening line; §7.1 of
   [RB-04](../rubrics/rb-04-reader-comprehension-rubric.md)).
2. **The scanning model is asserted.** No reader has been observed scanning this
   corpus; §5's simulation was performed by the system that wrote the page.
3. **The four sub-phases are asserted**, not derived.
4. **§5 tested one page**, chosen by the author, and found the claim holds
   "narrowly" — which is the kind of result a self-audit reliably produces.
5. **F2 has no control at all.** The estate's honesty makes its unverified claims
   more persuasive, and nothing in the corpus addresses this.
6. **No Arabic text**, while `prevailing_text: ar`.

## 8. Falsifier

> **IF FALSE.** Falsified if:
>
> - **(a)** a reader who scans a page reaches the same adverse conclusions as one
>   who reads it — which would defeat the whole scanning concern;
> - **(b)** a page's terminal state is shown to be scan-visible without being
>   tabulated or emphasised (§5.1);
> - **(c)** the variation rule is shown to be unobserved across the corpus —
>   **plausible and unchecked**; if most pages lead with Position, C3 is
>   systematically skipped;
> - **(d)** disclosure is shown **not** to raise trust in unrelated unverified
>   claims (§3.1, F2);
> - **(e)** a fifth sub-phase is identified.
>
> **(c) is runnable now, by anyone, with no tools**: list the opening section of
> twenty pages. If Position leads nearly all of them, the variation rule is
> decorative and the defence of C3 does not exist.

## 9. Obligation

**On an author (practice; this is a companion):**
- **Vary which section leads** ([ST-24](../standards/st-24-page-prose-standard.md)) —
  it is a defence of C3, not a style preference (§2.1).
- Make the worked instance's **terminal state visually prominent** (finding
  BX-002/1).
- Write adverse Limits, not flattering ones (§4, C3).
- Trace, never illustrate ([ST-17 §2.1](../standards/st-17-worked-instance-standard.md)).

**On the estate:**
- File **BX-002/1** as a ratification question for
  [ST-17](../standards/st-17-worked-instance-standard.md).
- **Run falsifier §8(c)** — count how many pages lead with Position. It needs no
  tools and would show whether the variation rule is real.
- Record **F2** as an unaddressed problem: the corpus's honesty amplifies trust in
  claims it has not verified, and nothing controls for it.
