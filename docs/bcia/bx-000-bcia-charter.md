# BX-000 — BCIA Charter

> **Authority.** `governing`, tier 3 (corpus layer). Subordinate to
> [ML-2.3](../license/the-maher-license.md),
> [HX-000](../harms/hx-000-the-fourteen-structural-harms.md) (tier 1a) and the
> [Charter](../corpus/mx-000-charter.md). BCIA is a **method**, not an authority;
> nothing here overrides a standard or a rubric.

> **Disclosure first.** BCIA has been named throughout this corpus since inception
> with **ten planned routes and zero written instruments** —
> [MX-004 Annex A](../corpus/mx-004-terms-of-top-levels.md) records it as the
> estate's largest empty division. This is the first BCIA document to exist. Nine
> remain unwritten, and the method's central claim is **E0** and, by the estate's
> own refusals, **untestable here** (§7).

## 1. Position

The estate's foundational premise, stated in
[BK-01's thesis](../registries/reg-03-book-registry.md), is that *a reader owes
themselves comprehension before reliance*. Every eight-part page
([ST-24](../standards/st-24-page-prose-standard.md)) and
[RB-04](../rubrics/rb-04-reader-comprehension-rubric.md)'s five dimensions serve
it.

But a premise about comprehension needs a **model of when comprehension happens**,
and the estate has been operating without one. BCIA supplies it:

> **B**efore · **C**ontact · **I**nternalisation · **A**fter — four phases in a
> reader's encounter with an instrument, each with different obligations on the
> corpus and different failure modes.

The claim is that these four are **distinguishable** and that **the corpus owes
different things in each**. That is the claim §8 asks a reader to falsify.

### 1.1 Why a method and not a standard

[MX-004 §2.2](../corpus/mx-004-terms-of-top-levels.md)'s tie-break rules would file
a comprehension *standard* under Standards. BCIA is filed under the corpus layer
because it is **descriptive** — it says what happens to a reader, not what an
author must do. Where BCIA implies a duty, the duty is discharged through an
existing standard (§4.2), never through BCIA itself.

## 2. The four phases

| Phase | What is happening | Corpus obligation | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B — Before** | Reader decides whether to read at all | State scope and audience honestly; abstract ≤ 120 words ([ST-16](../standards/st-16-audience-tier-standard.md), [ST-09 §2.1](../standards/st-09-summary-and-abstract-standard.md)) | Reader opens the wrong instrument, or is deterred from the right one |
| **C — Contact** | Reader is reading | Eight parts in order; register matched to tier ([ST-24](../standards/st-24-page-prose-standard.md)) | Fluency mistaken for understanding ([RB-04 §1.1](../rubrics/rb-04-reader-comprehension-rubric.md)) |
| **I — Internalisation** | Reader relates it to what they already hold | Cross-references, related blocks, worked instance ([ST-18](../standards/st-18-related-blocks-and-exit-pairs.md), [ST-17](../standards/st-17-worked-instance-standard.md)) | Reader absorbs the claims and not the limits |
| **A — After** | Reader acts, or is told the text changed | Obligation section; falsifier; version stamp; correction notice | **The estate cannot notify** ([MX-003 §5](../corpus/mx-003-privacy-notice.md)) |

### 2.1 The phases are not stages of a process

Important and easy to get wrong. A reader does not march B → C → I → A. They loop,
skip, and re-enter. BCIA's usefulness is not as a sequence but as **four distinct
sets of obligation that a single page must satisfy simultaneously.**

A page can be excellent at Contact and useless at Before: dense, well-formed,
eight parts, and no way for a reader to tell whether it is the page they need. That
is a common failure in this corpus and §5 traces one.

### 2.2 Phase A is where the estate is weakest

Phase A obligations divide:

| A-obligation | State |
|---|---|
| State what the reader must do (Obligation section) | **Met** — every instrument |
| State what would show it wrong (Falsifier) | **Met** |
| Let the reader tell whether the text is current | **Partly** — next review date in the page stamp ([MX-019 §6](../corpus/mx-019-sunset-and-wind-down.md)) |
| **Tell a reader who relied on it that it changed** | **Refused** — no address list ([MX-003 §5](../corpus/mx-003-privacy-notice.md)) |

The last is the estate's costliest refusal, and it is a **phase A** failure
specifically. A reader who completes B, C and I perfectly and then acts on the
instrument has no way of learning it was corrected. BCIA's contribution is to name
that as a phase failure rather than a policy footnote.

## 3. Definitions

| Term | Meaning here |
|---|---|
| **phase** | One of B, C, I, A |
| **phase obligation** | What the corpus owes a reader in that phase |
| **phase failure** | The corpus failing that obligation |
| **phase-blind page** | One satisfying Contact and no other phase |
| **re-entry** | A reader returning to a page after acting on it |

## 4. Mechanism

### 4.1 Auditing a page by phase

```
B: can a reader tell in 30 seconds whether this is their page?
C: eight parts present, register matched to declared tier?
I: worked instance, four related groups, limits section?
A: obligation stated, falsifier runnable, next review date visible?
```

Four questions. A page failing B is **phase-blind** even if C, I and A are perfect,
because a reader who never arrives is not served by what is inside.

### 4.2 BCIA imposes no new duties

Each phase obligation is discharged by an existing instrument:

| Phase | Discharged through |
|---|---|
| B | [ST-16](../standards/st-16-audience-tier-standard.md), [ST-09](../standards/st-09-summary-and-abstract-standard.md) |
| C | [ST-24](../standards/st-24-page-prose-standard.md), [SITE-02](../site/page-contract.md) |
| I | [ST-17](../standards/st-17-worked-instance-standard.md), [ST-18](../standards/st-18-related-blocks-and-exit-pairs.md), [ST-08](../standards/st-08-cross-reference-and-link-integrity.md) |
| A | [ST-04](../standards/st-04-falsifiability-and-claim-cards.md), [ST-05](../standards/st-05-decay-and-review-intervals.md), [MX-013](../corpus/mx-013-records-and-versioning.md) |

BCIA's value is **diagnostic**: it tells you *which* standard a page is failing, by
naming the phase where the reader is lost.

## 5. Worked instance

**Auditing one page by phase: [ST-15](../standards/st-15-amendment-and-versioning-standard.md).**

| Phase | Question | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| **B** | Can a reader tell in 30 seconds whether this is their page? | **Weak.** Title says "Amendment and Versioning Standard"; a reader wanting *"can I rename a file?"* cannot tell that §5 answers it. No abstract |
| **C** | Eight parts, register matched? | **Pass.** `depth: meta`, tier 3, all eight parts |
| **I** | Worked instance, related groups, limits? | **Strong.** The HX-000 trace exposed a genuine rule gap |
| **A** | Obligation, falsifier, review date? | **Pass.** Falsifier (a) fires and is disclosed |

**Terminal state: `result` — phase B is the weak one.**

### 5.1 The pattern generalises

Running the same four questions informally across this corpus gives a consistent
shape: **C, I and A are strong; B is weak nearly everywhere.**

The cause is structural, not careless. [ST-24](../standards/st-24-page-prose-standard.md)
mandates eight parts and **none of them is an abstract**. Position comes first, and
Position states *where the instrument sits in the ladder* — which is a phase-I
orientation (relating it to what the reader holds) delivered in the phase-B slot,
where the reader has not yet decided to read.

So the estate's own prose standard is **structurally phase-B-blind**, and every
page inherits it. [ST-09 §2.1](../standards/st-09-summary-and-abstract-standard.md)
defines an abstract (≤ 120 words, no obligations) and marks it *"encouraged"* —
never required.

Filed per [ST-17 W6](../standards/st-17-worked-instance-standard.md):

> **Finding BX-000/1.** [ST-24](../standards/st-24-page-prose-standard.md)'s eight
> parts serve phases C, I and A and leave phase B unserved. The available remedy is
> to make the [ST-09](../standards/st-09-summary-and-abstract-standard.md) abstract
> **mandatory** on every instrument above tier 1, placed before Position. This is
> a change to a tier 5 standard and is **not** within BCIA's authority to make
> (§1.1); it is filed as a ratification question.

That is BCIA earning its place on its first page: a systematic weakness invisible
to the eight-part recipe itself, found by asking a question the recipe does not
ask.

## 7. Limits

1. **BCIA's central claim is E0.** That the four phases are distinguishable and
   carry different obligations is asserted from reasoning, not observation.
2. **It cannot be validated here.** Validation needs data about readers, refused at
   [REG-06 `DSET-R01`](../registries/reg-06-dataset-registry.md) and
   [MX-023](../corpus/mx-023-attention-and-human-sovereignty.md). The estate has
   chosen a refusal over the evidence, exactly as it did for its licence's central
   premise ([REG-06 §5.1](../registries/reg-06-dataset-registry.md)).
3. **No comprehension literature was surveyed** — the same gap
   [RB-04 §6.5](../rubrics/rb-04-reader-comprehension-rubric.md) records. Four
   phases are asserted, not derived.
4. **Nine of ten BCIA instruments are absent.**
5. **Written by the system that wrote the corpus it audits**
   ([MX-017 C1](../corpus/mx-017-conflicts-of-interest.md)); §5.1's finding is a
   self-criticism, which is more credible than self-praise and still not
   independent.
6. **No Arabic text**, while `prevailing_text: ar`.

## 8. Falsifier

> **IF FALSE.** Falsified if:
>
> - **(a)** the four phases are shown **not** to be distinguishable — that a page
>   serving Contact well necessarily serves Before well. §5 exhibits a
>   counter-example; one page is not a demonstration;
> - **(b)** a page failing phase B is shown to serve readers as well as one that
>   does not;
> - **(c)** a phase obligation is found that no existing standard discharges —
>   which would mean BCIA **does** create duties, contrary to §4.2;
> - **(d)** a fifth phase is identified;
> - **(e)** §5.1's claim that the corpus is systematically weak at B is shown wrong
>   by an audit of ten pages.
>
> **(e) is runnable by any reader in an hour** — four questions, ten pages. **(d)
> is the more interesting**: four phases were asserted by a system that has never
> observed a reader.

## 9. Obligation

**On an author:**
- Audit by all four phases, not only Contact (§4.1).
- Serve phase B explicitly: state in the opening lines who this page is for and
  what question it answers.
- Never treat BCIA as authority; discharge phase obligations through the named
  standards (§4.2).
- Never present the four phases as a sequence (§2.1).

**On the estate:**
- File finding **BX-000/1** as a ratification question: should the
  [ST-09](../standards/st-09-summary-and-abstract-standard.md) abstract become
  mandatory above tier 1 (§5.1)?
- Keep BCIA's central claim labelled **E0** and state that the estate's own
  refusals prevent testing it (§7.2).
- Write the remaining nine BCIA instruments, or record the family as deferred in
  [GM-02](../00-governance-map/document-index.md) rather than leaving ten routes
  planned and one written.
- Do not instrument readers to validate BCIA
  ([MX-023](../corpus/mx-023-attention-and-human-sovereignty.md)).
