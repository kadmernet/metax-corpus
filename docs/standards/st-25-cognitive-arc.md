# ST-25 · The Cognitive Arc and Series Architecture

> **Authority.** `operative-form`, tier 5. Parent policy: [MX-026](../corpus/mx-026-editorial-and-evidence.md).
> Companion standards: [ST-03](st-03-limits-and-bridge.md) (Limits and Bridge),
> [ST-26](st-26-unit-economics-and-kill-criteria.md) (kill criteria and unit economics). Generator:
> [AG-021](../agents/ag-021-series-generation-charter.md).

> **Disclosure first.** **No lesson in the estate follows this arc**, because no lesson exists. And
> the arc's central claim — that six beats in this order produce judgement — has **no evidence
> behind it**. The source specification requires this page to say so, and it does in §7.

---

## 1. Position

A curriculum is an argument about the order in which understanding must be built. The arc makes
the argument explicit and uniform so it can be **criticised as one object** rather than
rediscovered in every lesson.

## 2. Definition — the six beats

| Beat | Question | Output | Target length `[VERIFY]` |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 **What is** | What is the object, operatively? | A definition with a boundary | ~10% |
| 2 **Why** | Why does it matter, and to whom? | A stated stake, including who loses | ~10% |
| 3 **How** | How does it work? | Mechanism, step by step | ~25% |
| 4 **Lab** | Can you make it happen? | A guided exercise with a checkable result | ~25% |
| 5 **Artifact** | What do you keep? | A reusable artefact the learner owns | ~15% |
| 6 **Scenario Assessment** | Can you judge when it fails? | A scenario with a decision and reasons | ~15% |

Proportions are proposals; the source states beats and purposes, not lengths (V-14 extended).

## 3. Mechanism — series architecture

- **Default cadence: 12 courses × 6 lessons.** Minimum six courses. Deviation must be stated on
  the series page with its reason.
- **Mandatory in every series:** Limits module (four classes) **immediately before** the Bridge
  lesson; Objections block; 90-day capstone.
- **Capstone contract:** pre-registered hypothesis, explicit **null**, decision rule, kill
  criteria, unit economics where commercial ([ST-26](st-26-unit-economics-and-kill-criteria.md));
  public post-mortem regardless of outcome.
- **Companion rails** embedded, not taught separately: compliance · accessibility ·
  decay-awareness · falsifiability · kill criteria · unit economics. For each, the series must
  show the **artefact the rail forces**.

## 4. Worked instance — a lesson skeleton (S08, causal measurement)

1. *What is:* an A/B test is a randomised comparison; its boundary is that it answers "did this
   cause that here", not "why".
2. *Why:* decisions made on correlational dashboards; who loses is the user shown the worse arm.
3. *How:* randomisation unit, sample size, pre-registration.
4. *Lab:* run a simulated test on supplied data; the checkable result is the confidence interval.
5. *Artifact:* a pre-registration template the learner keeps.
6. *Scenario:* a stakeholder wants to stop early at p = 0.04 on day 3. Decide, with reasons.

## 5. Failure modes

Beat 6 collapses into a quiz; beat 4 is a video; Limits appended after the Bridge; capstone
without a null.

## 6. Limits

The arc structures a lesson; it cannot make content correct. It suits procedural and analytic
subjects better than contemplative or historical ones — the Ascent rungs, taught as history, may
need deviation.

## 7. Falsifier — and the evidence gap

**The arc is a design choice, not a proven method.** `[VERIFY]` — the studies that would settle
it: controlled comparison of arc-structured vs conventional lessons on delayed transfer tasks
(the D3/D5 dimensions of RB-01). None has been run or cited. The arc is **wrong** if such a
comparison shows no transfer advantage.

## 8. Obligation

Every series declares compliance or deviation per beat. RB-06 checks presence.

## 9. Macro register

Most online courses end at beat 4: a demonstration and a feeling of competence. The arc's
distinctive claim is that **a lesson is not finished until the learner has judged a case where
the method fails**. That is the difference between training operators and training people who
can be trusted with a method.
