# Determinations Procedure

> **Authority.** `operative-form` under ML-2.3 Parts D and G. Records are kept at
> [REG-11](../registries/reg-11-determinations-registry.md).

> **Disclosure first.** **Zero Determinations have been issued.** The procedure has never run. Its
> fairness guarantees assume a Steward and at least one independent reviewer; only the Steward
> exists ([DEF-031](../00-governance-map/defect-register.md#def-031)).

---

## 1. Position

A licence that can declare another licence or another Work non-conforming holds a power that is
easily abused: to suppress a competitor, a critic, or a fork. Part D and §G.5 answer by making the
power **procedural** — reasons, publication, a right of reply, prospectivity, a cure window. This
document is the procedure a reader can hold the Steward to.

## 2. Definition — two kinds

| Kind | Question | Outcomes |
|---|---|---|
| **Compatibility Determination** (Part D) | Is Subject License X compatible with ML? | ML-Compatible · ML-Conditionally-Compatible · ML-Incompatible |
| **Charter Conformity Determination** (Part G) | Does Work of Foundational Character Y conform to the Charter Conditions? | Conforming · Non-conforming in stated respects |

## 3. Mechanism (micro) — nine steps

| # | Step | Deadline | Published? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Request filed (by anyone) or Steward's own motion, stating the question | — | Yes, on receipt |
| 2 | Conflict check under [MX-017](../corpus/mx-017-conflicts-of-interest.md); recusal or disclosure | 5 working days | Yes |
| 3 | Affected party notified with the draft question | 10 working days | Yes |
| 4 | Analysis against the four-test rubric (D.2) — *Sidq*, *Adl*, *Amanah*, *Darar* — with pros **and** cons | — | Yes, at decision |
| 5 | Draft Determination sent to affected party | — | No (draft) |
| 6 | Reply window | 90 days (§G.6) | Reply published if given |
| 7 | Steward's reasoned reply **engaging the merits** of the response | 30 days | Yes |
| 8 | Final Determination, signed ([ML-OPS-1](registry-and-mirrors.md)) | — | Yes, all mirrors |
| 9 | Effect — **prospective only**, from publication; good-faith prior acts unaffected | — | — |

**Core Riba rule.** A Subject License that requires or authorises Core Riba (§C.9.2) fails the
*Amanah*/*Darar* test and is ML-Incompatible (Part D). That outcome is still produced through all
nine steps; the rule fixes the answer, not the process.

**§G.5 humility clause.** A Determination issued to suppress dissent, criticism, or competition,
or to entrench the Steward's advantage, is **void**. Any reader may raise §G.5 at step 6.

## 4. Worked instance (hypothetical, labelled)

*Hypothetical — no such request exists.* An academy forks ST-24 and relicenses it under a Derived
License that drops §C.10.5 (impersonation). Step 4 finds the Derived License fails §E.6(g).
Outcome: ML-Incompatible **as a Derived License**, with the reason, and a note that the fork may
continue under its own name without claiming to be ML-based. The academy's reply (step 6) argues
the omission was accidental; the Steward's reply (step 7) accepts and offers a cure: restore
§C.10.5 within the window, and the Determination is withdrawn before effect.

## 5. Failure modes

- **Steward decides his own case** — structurally unavoidable today; must be disclosed on the
  Determination's face.
- **Unpublished negative Determinations** — reads as arbitrary; publication is a Part D duty.
- **Retroactive effect** — forbidden by §G.6; would make the licence commercially unusable.

## 6. Limits

The procedure produces a reasoned position, not a judgment. No court is bound by it.

## 7. Falsifier

Wrong if a party can be adversely affected by a Determination without having seen the draft, had
90 days, and received a published reply engaging its argument.

## 8. Obligation

**Steward:** follow §3 in full for the first Determination, including step 2 disclosure that no
independent reviewer exists. **Agents:** may prepare step-4 analysis drafts only under
[AG-014](../agents/ag-014-roster-and-role-charters.md) CRITIC role; may never issue.

## 9. Macro register

Meta-licences decide what counts as "the same" licence, which is a power over the whole commons
built on it. Publishing the reasoning, including the arguments against, turns that power from
**gatekeeping into jurisprudence** — a body of reasons others can cite, contest, and outgrow.
