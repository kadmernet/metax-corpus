# Designation List — Works of Foundational Character

**List version DL-2** · Maintained under [ML-2.3](ml-2-3.md) · Published in the Registry.

> **Re-based to ML-2.3 at DL-2 (2026‑10‑01).** DL-1 was maintained under
> [ML-2.1 §2.2](ml-2-1.md#22-defined-terms). The clause references in §1 and §3 below still cite
> ML-2.1 section numbers, and that is **deliberate rather than stale**: DL-1's designations were
> made under those clauses, and rewriting the citations would obscure which text a designation was
> made under. Rows added at DL-2 cite ML-2.3. Where the two instruments' Part G differ, ML-2.3
> governs and the divergence is a defect to be filed under §E.9.
>
> **The four rows added at DL-2** are the instruments ML-2.3 itself incorporates or requires, and
> without them the list contradicted the licence: an instrument incorporated by reference into a
> Tier 1 governing text cannot be undesignated.

> **Status: RATIFICATION PENDING.** This list is drafted and is not yet ratified by the
> Steward. Until ratification, designation for any Work below should be treated as asserted
> rather than settled. Tracked at
> [RAT-06](../00-governance-map/open-questions-and-ratifications.md#rat-06--ratify-the-designation-list).

## 1. What designation does

A Work on this list is a **Work of Foundational Character**, which means
[Part G](ml-2-1.md#part-g--the-charter-layer) applies to it. Three consequences follow.

1. The Work must satisfy the Charter Conditions of §G.2 in its own substance and operation —
   truthful establishment, just ordering, transparent foundation, trustworthy stewardship, and
   non-harm including non-Riba.
2. The Work may be the subject of a **Charter Conformity Determination**, which operates
   prospectively with a 90-day cure window under [§G.6](ml-2-1.md#g6--prospectivity-and-cure).
3. The Work attracts the stronger attribution regime of
   [§6-bis](ml-2-1.md#6-bis--attribution-integrity-for-foundational-works) and the machine
   provenance obligation of [§C.10.2](ml-2-1.md#c10--machine-learning-model-training-and-synthetic-derivation),
   both of which apply specifically to foundational Works.

## 2. Why there is a list at all

Designation could have been left to per-work judgement in the notice. It is not, for two
reasons pulling in the same direction.

**Predictability for adopters.** Part G's supremacy over the direct-license layer makes the
charter layer genuinely expensive to accept. A party evaluating adoption needs to know, before
committing, whether a given Work carries it. A list answers that in one lookup; a
judgement-call standard requires them to guess.

**Bounding the scope creep.** If nearly everything the Steward publishes is designated
foundational, the charter layer's supremacy makes adoption frightening; if too little is
designated, the layer is decorative. Neither failure is recoverable after the fact, and the
list is what makes the balance visible and criticisable.

The list and the two-of-five test in §2.2 are not alternatives — they compose. **The list is
the operative designation. The test governs disputes about Works not on the list.**

## 4. What is deliberately NOT designated

Designation is not a compliment and non-designation is not a demotion. The following are
published under [MX-012](../corpus/mx-012-licensing-and-reuse.md) without Part G, because each
operates *within* an order established elsewhere rather than establishing one:

- Individual course, lesson and capstone material, including Ascent rung pages.
- Site templates, component implementations, CSS, and build scripts.
- Machine artefacts — `catalog.json`, `credentials.json`, `lexicon.json`, `tree.json`,
  `status.json`, `defects.json` — which are *renderings* of designated Works, not the Works.
- Changelog entries, corrections entries, audit findings and defect records. These are records
  *about* the order and are protected by [MX-013](../corpus/mx-013-records-and-versioning.md)
  rather than by Part G.
- Marketing copy, press descriptions, and the press kit.
- The [plain-language companion](ml-2-1-plain-language.md), which is expressly non-operative.
- **[HX-002, the Harm → Instrument Map](../harms/hx-002-harm-instrument-map.md)**, and
  **[RB-05, the Harm Review Gate](../rubrics/rb-05-harm-review-gate.md)**. See §4.1 — these two
  are the closest calls on the list and the reasoning is given rather than assumed.
- **[The Scale Gate worksheet](scale-gate.md)**, which is a *rendering* of ML-2.3 §H.2-bis into a
  form. The clause is designated by row 14; the worksheet inherits the licence and not the
  designation, on the same reasoning as the JSON artefacts above.
- **[SITE-01…SITE-04](../site/route-manifest.md)**, **[ST-22](../standards/st-22-platform-and-persistence.md)**,
  **[ST-23](../standards/st-23-affiliate-and-referral.md)**, **[ST-24](../standards/st-24-page-prose-standard.md)**
  and **[DS-07](../design/ds-07-navigation-geometry.md)**. These govern how *this* estate is built
  and are not offered for adoption as an order. ST-23 is the interesting exclusion: it is an
  original design with no upstream source, which might argue for designation — and it is excluded
  precisely because an agent-designed instrument governing by default should not also carry Part
  G's supremacy ([RAT-23](../00-governance-map/open-questions-and-ratifications.md#rat-23)).

The distinction matters most for the third row. A JSON artefact generated from designated
front matter could be argued into §2.2(ii) on the ground that it carries a taxonomy. The
Steward's position is that a rendering inherits the licence of its source but not its
designation, because designating the rendering would make every build output a charter
instrument and every stale cache a conformity question.

### 4.1 The two closest calls at DL-2, and why both fall outside

**[HX-002, the Harm → Instrument Map](../harms/hx-002-harm-instrument-map.md).** The argument for
designation is real: it is a taxonomy, it maps a designated register to designated instruments,
and it is cited in the estate's own compliance reasoning. The argument against is decisive. HX-002
is a **record about the order**, not part of it — its substantive content is an assessment of what
this estate currently does, including three harms it fails and two it has not examined. That is a
finding, and findings are protected by [MX-013](../corpus/mx-013-records-and-versioning.md) rather
than by Part G, on exactly the footing as audit findings and defect records.

There is a second reason and it is the stronger one. Designating a self-assessment would make the
estate's own unflattering findings subject to a Charter Conformity Determination — which would
create a standing incentive to write them more carefully. A register of one's own failures must be
cheap to write honestly, and Part G's supremacy makes nothing cheap.

**[RB-05, the Harm Review Gate](../rubrics/rb-05-harm-review-gate.md).** Structurally similar to
HX-001, which *is* designated at row 15, so the distinction needs stating. HX-001 is an
**assessment standard** — it tells any party how to assess any deployment, and is offered for
adoption. RB-05 is a **workflow gate**: it specifies where in *this estate's* process the
assessment is enforced, and its §5 prohibitions bind this estate's own reviewers. A gate is
institutional plumbing, not an order offered to others.

The line the list is drawing at DL-2, stated so it can be applied to the next candidate: **a Work
that tells a third party how to do something is designable; a Work that records what this estate
found, or where this estate enforces something, is not.**

## 5. Amendment of this list

- Additions and removals are made by numbered release with reasons, and are **prospective**.
- A Work removed from the list ceases to be foundational prospectively; Determinations already
  issued stand as historical record and cease to be capable of amendment.
- Removal is not a withdrawal of the Work and does not affect any grant.
- Every change to this list appears in [License Version History](version-history.md) and in
  [Changelog](../00-governance-map/changelog.md).
- A Work may not be added retroactively in order to make conduct that has already occurred
  non-conforming. That would defeat [§G.6](ml-2-1.md#g6--prospectivity-and-cure) by another
  route, and any determination resting on such an addition is void under
  [§G.5](ml-2-1.md#g5-humility-clause).

## 6. What is unresolved on the face of this list

Three things, stated here rather than left to be discovered.

**Ratification is still pending.** DL-1 was drafted and unratified; DL-2 adds four rows and is
equally unratified ([RAT-06](../00-governance-map/open-questions-and-ratifications.md#rat-06--ratify-the-designation-list)).
Every designation on this list should be read as **asserted rather than settled**, including the
four added today. A list that grows while its ratification stays pending is accumulating asserted
authority, and that is worth noticing rather than normalising.

**Row 13 designates a text that is one-third written.** HX-000 exists at eleven of twenty-five
harms ([DEF-021](../00-governance-map/defect-register.md#def-021), ML‑D‑008). It is designated
anyway, because ML-2.3 §A.10 incorporates it and the alternative — leaving the licence's operative
register undesignated until convenient — is worse. But a Charter Conformity Determination against
an incomplete Work is not presently answerable, and no party should rely on row 13 until the
register is complete.

**Rows 1 and 14 both designate the licence.** Row 1 says "all versions"; row 14 names ML-2.3 and
Part H specifically. This is redundant by design and is **not** a numbering error: row 14 exists so
that a reader searching for Part H finds a designation entry rather than inferring one from "all
versions." Under [ST-01 Rule 2](../standards/st-01-identifier-and-url-grammar.md#3-rule-2--numbers-are-immortal)
neither row may now be renumbered, and the redundancy is recorded here so a future editor does not
"tidy" it into a defect.
