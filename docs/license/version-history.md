# The Maher License — Version History

**What this instrument is.** The record of every version of the Maher License, what changed, and
**why** — with one gap declared on its face rather than concealed.

**Why this document is `authority: record` rather than `governing`.** It governs nothing. It is
evidence about the instrument's history, and a version history that acquired governing force
would become a second place where the licence's meaning is fixed. Where this document and
[ML-2.3](ml-2-3.md) disagree, ML-2.3 governs and the disagreement is a defect to be filed under
§E.9.

## 2. What each version did, and why

### ML-1.0 — the original grant

A permissive grant with **ethical conditions attached to conduct**: do not deceive, do not
oppress, do not misrepresent authorship. Its innovation was modest and real — conditions that were
neither copyleft nor field-of-use restrictions, but norms of conduct toward persons.

Its weakness was structural and was inherited by every version until 2.3: **conduct conditions
catch actors who choose to do harm and miss systems that produce harm nobody chose.**

### ML-1.1 — clarifications

Definitional repairs and clarified notice requirements. **No new conditions.** Recorded here
because a version history that omits its uneventful releases teaches a reader that every version
was consequential, which makes the consequential ones harder to see.

### ML-2.0 — the restructuring

Reorganised into lettered Parts — definitions, grant, conditions, notices, governance,
breach, interpretation — and added a governance layer: a steward, a determinations procedure, a
registry.

**Why the restructuring mattered more than it looks.** A licence that grows by accretion becomes
unciteable: a condition buried in a paragraph cannot be referred to precisely, and a licence whose
clauses cannot be cited cannot be argued about. The Parts structure made the instrument
**addressable**, which is the same property the estate later insisted on for its
[routes](../site/routing-law.md) and for the same reason.

### ML-2.1 — riba, redundancy, and anti-capture

Three additions, each closing a specific hole.

**§C.9, Core Riba as an absolute prohibition**, defined by four cumulative elements rather than by
name — so that the prohibition attaches to a structure rather than to a label an instrument can be
renamed around. This four-element form later became the estate's standard analytical shape for
money questions; [ST-23 §2](../standards/st-23-affiliate-and-referral.md) is modelled on it
directly.

**§E.7, registry continuity** — canonical text, version history and all determinations in at least
three independent publicly readable locations, one content-addressed, one jurisdictionally
distinct and outside the steward's operational control. The reasoning is that a licence retrievable
from only one place is a licence its steward can quietly alter, and §E.4's reliance protection is
untestable without redundancy. Still unbuilt: [B-02](../00-governance-map/launch-blockers.md).

**§E.8, anti-capture** — the steward may not charge for compliance nor grant unpublished
exemptions. This is the clause that makes the estate's own unissued
[Part H Statement](part-h-statement.md) a governance problem rather than a scheduling one: a
steward who omits its own Statement has granted itself the exemption §E.8(b) says does not exist.

### ML-2.3 — Part H, and a change of kind

**Not an addition. A change in what the instrument is.**

ML-2.1 conditioned permission on **conduct**. Part H conditions permission on **structure**: on
harm that no actor intends, that arises from how a system is built and financed, and that is
invisible to the party causing it because *the metric certifying success is the instrument of the
injury*. A licence cannot acquire that by amendment, because it changes the answer to "what is
this document for."

**What arrived with it:**

| Device | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Twenty-five harms, six strata** | I Cognitive (HX‑01…03) · II Relational (HX‑04…06) · III Economic (HX‑07…09, 13) · IV Material (HX‑10…12, 14) · **V Reflexive (HX‑15…19)** · **VI Successor (HX‑20…25)** |
| **Eight absolutes, §H.3(a)** | Bind at every scale including private use. (vii) developmental substitution is **disjunctive**; the rest are cumulative on the §C.9.2 model |
| **§H.1-bis, four roles** | Author · Integrator · Operator · Commissioner — no role may be left blank |
| **§H.2-bis, the Scale Gate** | Tier 0 Personal / 1 Small / 2 Substantial / 3 Systemic. [Worksheet](scale-gate.md) |
| **§F.4, condition/covenant split** | Conditions (§C.5, §C.9, §H.3(a)(i)–(viii), §B.5(b)) terminate automatically; everything else is covenant with 30/90-day cure at §F.5 and automatic reinstatement at §F.6 |
| **§H.16, the Part H Statement** | And §H.16.3: **an all-clear Statement is presumptively non-compliant** and is evidence of HX‑15 |
| **§§H.17–H.31** | Non-theatre, consent integrity, authorship of record, chain of responsibility, proportionate imposition, language parity, channel parity, developmental default, agent constraint, corpus hygiene, reversibility, custody, substitution disclosure, non-participation, interaction |

**Two structural repairs worth naming separately.** The harm identifiers were renamed `H{n}` →
`HX-{nn}`, which repaired a **citation collision** between harms and duties that the estate had
been about to solve with a local convention of its own — withdrawn at
[C-10](../00-governance-map/corrections-log.md#c-10) once the upstream fix arrived. And §H.16.3
inverted the incentive of the whole compliance apparatus: every other disclosure regime rewards a
clean return, and this one treats a clean return as evidence of the harm it names.

**The strata V and VI are the genuinely new intellectual content.** Strata I–IV describe harm to
present persons; **V (Reflexive)** describes harm produced *by the act of governing* — compliance
theatre, metric capture, the audit that certifies what it was built to certify. **VI (Successor)**
describes harm to people who do not yet exist and cannot object. Both are directed at the estate
itself as much as at any licensee, and §H.16.3 is Stratum V made operative against its own author.

## 3. ML-2.2 — declared, never published

**This is a defect on the face of the estate's Tier 1 instrument and it is stated here rather than
smoothed over.**

ML-2.3 declares `supersedes: "ML-2.2"`. Its §E.3 preserves ML‑2.2 grants until **2027‑03‑31**. Its
changelog describes itself as the repair of six defects **in ML‑2.2**. The estate has never held,
received or published an ML‑2.2 text. The published predecessor is [ML‑2.1](ml-2-1.md).

**Consequence:** §E.3's transition provision has no referent, so no licensee can determine which
version governs an existing grant, and **no party may rely on §E.3** until this resolves. Filed as
licence defect **ML‑D‑007** and reconciliation entry
[R-38](../00-governance-map/reconciliation-ledger.md#r-38--the-ml-22-gap).

**Three options, and one of them is refused on principle.**

| Option | Cost |
|---|---|
| Publish a retrospective ML-2.2 | **Refused.** See below |
| Re-base 2.3's references onto 2.1 | Amends a governing instrument the Curator supplied |
| Publish 2.3 with the gap declared | A declared defect on the estate's highest-tier document |

**Option three was taken.** Option one is refused and the refusal is the substantive content of
this section: drafting a 2.2 whose defects conveniently match 2.3's repairs would be the estate
**fabricating its own history inside the instrument that defines *Ṣidq*** — the norm ML-2.3 states
as *"do not build the machine that makes the distinction between lying and telling the truth
unrecoverable."* A version history is exactly such a machine when it is convenient.

The gap is therefore permanent in this table unless the Curator supplies a text that already
exists. It resolves at
[RAT-14](../00-governance-map/open-questions-and-ratifications.md#rat-14--does-ml-22-exist), and
the route `/license/falsification/ml-2-2/` is specified to **state the gap** rather than to
display a document — building it as an empty archive page would convert an honest defect into a
quiet one.

## 4. The trajectory, read as one movement

Six versions in one direction, and it is worth naming because it is not the direction most licence
lines travel.

**1.0 → 2.1 broadened the subject of protection.** From *the author's interests* to *conduct toward
persons* to *the structure of financial arrangements*.

**2.1 → 2.3 broadened the subject of accountability**, and this is the sharper turn. Earlier
versions asked *what did you do*. Part H asks *what does your system produce, whether or not you
intended it, and what have you measured*. That is a shift from a conduct standard to a
**structural** one, and it is the reason the instrument acquired a scale gate: structural duties
are investigative, and investigative duties must be proportionate or they will be ignored.

**The cost of the trajectory, stated honestly.** Each version made the licence harder to comply
with and harder to enforce. ML-1.0 could be complied with by not lying. ML-2.3 requires reading a
twenty-five-harm register, assessing a system against it, and publishing an unflattering result.
Its two central devices — the condition/covenant split and the Scale Gate — are **untested in any
forum** and its drafter says so
([RAT-18](../00-governance-map/open-questions-and-ratifications.md#rat-18), ML‑D‑010).

`status: canonical` records the instrument's standing in **the estate's own governance**. It is not
a representation that ML-2.3 has been tested anywhere, and no page in this corpus may render it as
one.

## 5. What this history does not contain

**Signatures.** ML-2.1 still carries `[STEWARD NAME / ORGANIZATION]`, `[REGISTRY URL]`, `[DATE]`
and `[FPR]` unfilled, and the text is unsigned
([B-01](../00-governance-map/launch-blockers.md),
[RAT-01](../00-governance-map/open-questions-and-ratifications.md#rat-01--fill-and-sign-the-license-placeholders)).
An unsigned governing charter has no author, which makes every downstream accountability claim
unsupported — including this history's.

**A registry.** §E.7 requires three independent mirrors. None exists
([B-02](../00-governance-map/launch-blockers.md)). Until they do, this file is the *only* copy of
the version history, held by the steward, in the steward's own repository — precisely the
single-point-of-custody arrangement §E.7 was written to forbid.

**Counsel review.** None, in any jurisdiction, of any version.

**Determinations.** No determination has been issued under Parts D and G, so the interpretive
record is empty and every clause's meaning rests on its text alone.

**Withdrawals.** No harm has been narrowed or withdrawn under §E.5. The register is nine days old,
so this is expected rather than damning — but it is the number to watch. *A register whose entries
have never been narrowed is presumptively a doctrine*, and this table is where the first
withdrawal will be recorded.

## 6. Obligation

**For a licensee:** identify your version. If your grant predates ML-2.3 you cannot currently
determine your transition position, because §E.3 refers to a text that does not exist (§3). Until
RAT-14 resolves, the estate's position is that **no party is disadvantaged by relying on ML-2.1's
terms** for an existing grant, and this sentence is published so it can be relied upon.

**For the estate:** resolve RAT-14; build the §E.7 mirrors so this history is not custodied by its
own subject; obtain counsel review before representing ML-2.3 as binding on a third party; and
record the first §E.5 withdrawal here when it comes.

**Under ML-2.3:** §H.19 (authorship of record) is why the ML-2.2 gap is declared rather than
filled; §H.26 (corpus hygiene) is why superseded versions are retained rather than deleted; §H.28
(custody and horizon) is why §5 names this file's own custody problem.
