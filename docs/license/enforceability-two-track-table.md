# Two-Track Enforceability Table

> **What this document is.** A statement of enforcement posture, required by
> [ML-2.1 §1.5](ml-2-1.md#15-relationship-to-secular-and-international-law-reinforcing-character)
> and carried forward under ML-2.3. It creates no obligation and relieves no one of any. Where it
> appears to differ from the License, the [License](ml-2-3.md) governs and the difference is a
> defect to be filed under §E.9.

> **Re-based to ML-2.3, 2026‑10‑01.** §3's rows cite ML-2.1 clause numbers because those
> assertions were made against that text and rewriting the citations would obscure which
> instrument each posture was stated under. **§6 is new** and carries the Part H rows, including
> the condition/covenant split at §F.4 — which is the single most consequential enforceability
> change in the licence's history and the one this table previously did not address at all.

## 1. Why this table exists

A reader of [ML-2.1](ml-2-1.md) can reach the end of Part C without being able to answer one
question: which of these conditions could actually be enforced against me, and which are moral
statements the Steward has chosen to make?

That ambiguity has a predictable commercial consequence. An adopter's counsel reads Part C as
an open-ended use restriction, prices in litigation exposure they cannot bound, and declines.
The License is then rejected not for its ethics but for the cost of determining its
consequences — and the Steward would have learned nothing, because the refusal arrives as
silence.

Publishing the posture per clause removes the ambiguity in the direction of honesty rather
than in the direction of advantage. It is required by *Sidq*, and it is the reason no adopter
should have to reverse-engineer our enforcement position from clause structure.

## 2. The two tracks, defined

**Track 1 — condition of use (contractual).** The Steward asserts the provision as a limit on
what you may *do* with the Work, enforceable as a condition of the grant to the extent the
forum's law allows. Breach can void rights in the Work.

**Track 2 — condition of representation and association.** The Steward asserts the provision
as a limit on what you may *say* about your relationship to the Work: whether you may call it
ML-licensed, describe it as compatible, or associate your work with this License and its
Steward. This track survives in nearly every jurisdiction because it is trademark-adjacent
rather than a use restriction.

**Track 3 — moral norm, no enforcement claim.** The Steward states the norm and asserts no
mechanism. It is published because *Sidq* requires the Steward to distinguish what he will
enforce from what he merely believes.

A provision may occupy more than one track. §C.9 deliberately occupies both 1 and 2, and the
second is what makes it operative everywhere.

## 4. Read this honestly

Where a row says **"reinforcing only"**, the Steward is telling you that a court may decline to
enforce that provision as a limit on your conduct, and that he has no intention of pretending
otherwise. Sections C.1–C.5 mostly restate conduct that is already unlawful; where a forum
enforces the underlying law, the License adds a remedy, and where it does not, the License
does not manufacture one.

What survives everywhere is the right to say your work is ML-licensed. **That right is the
sanction.** A party who breaches an ethical condition in a forum that will not enforce it as a
use restriction does not thereby acquire the right to describe their work as governed by,
compatible with, or derived from this License — and for most adopters that representation is
the thing of value.

Two further honesty points, stated because omitting them would defeat the purpose:

- **§C.9's second track is the load-bearing one.** The Steward's expectation is that the use
  restriction will be enforceable in some forums and not others, and that the representation
  limit will be enforceable in nearly all. That is why
  [§C.9.6](ml-2-1.md#c96-non-severability-corrected) makes the two severable from each other.
- **§G.5 runs against the Steward.** A determination issued in bad faith is void. If you
  believe one has been, the route is [Determinations Procedure §6](determinations-procedure.md#6-challenging-a-determination),
  and your response is published alongside the determination whether or not it succeeds.

## 6. Part H — the rows this table previously lacked

Part H changed what kind of instrument the licence is: ML-2.1 conditioned permission on **conduct**,
Part H conditions it on **structure**. That change has enforceability consequences the three-track
model can describe but not flatter, and the honest summary is that **Part H's investigative duties
are the weakest track-1 claims in the licence and its absolutes are the strongest.**

### 6.1 The condition/covenant split (§F.4) — read this first

§F.4 divides the entire licence in two, and the division is what makes the rest of this section
legible.

| Class | Provisions | Effect of breach |
|---|---|---|
| **Conditions** | §C.5, §C.9, **§H.3(a)(i)–(viii)**, §B.5(b) | **Automatic termination** of the grant |
| **Covenants** | Everything else, including all of Part H's disclosure and assessment duties | Breach sounds in contract; 30/90-day cure at §F.5; automatic reinstatement at §F.6 |

**Why this matters more than any single row below.** A licensee who fails to publish a Part H
Statement has breached a **covenant** — they have not lost their rights, and they have a cure
window. A licensee who breaches one of the eight absolutes has breached a **condition**, and the
grant has already terminated by operation of the clause, before anyone writes to them.

The estate states plainly which way the risk runs for an adopter: **the covenant track is
substantially safer than adopters typically assume, and the condition track is less forgiving than
they typically assume.** Counsel reading Part H as an open-ended termination risk has
misread §F.4; counsel reading the absolutes as negotiable has misread §H.3(a).

### 6.3 Four honesty points about Part H's enforceability

**The register is not a prohibition.** [HX-000](../harms/) *describes* twenty-five structural
harms; it does not forbid them. Only §H.3(a)'s eight absolutes prohibit, and only they terminate.
A licensee whose Statement records four harms as *unremedied* is in **good standing** under this
licence — they have complied with §H.16 by disclosing honestly. This is the most commonly
misunderstood feature of Part H and it is stated here because misunderstanding it would cause an
adopter to price a risk that does not exist.

**§H.16's real sanction is track 2, as with §C.9.** The Steward's expectation is that a court may
decline to enforce a duty to publish a self-assessment as a limit on conduct, and that the
representation limit will be enforceable in nearly all forums. **A party that does not publish a
Statement does not thereby acquire the right to describe its work as ML-2.3-licensed** — and for
most adopters that representation is the thing of value.

**§H.19 is the strongest covenant in Part H**, because it is attribution-adjacent. Attribution
integrity is enforced in more jurisdictions and more readily than any ethical use condition, so a
duty framed as *who is the recorded author of this assertion* inherits a stronger posture than a
duty framed as *what must you disclose about your optimisation*. Licensees should expect §H.19 to
be the Part H duty most likely to be tested.

**§H.21 is a standard, not a rule, and the estate says so.** Proportionate imposition asks whether
a burden is proportionate to the benefit and to the affected party's ability to decline. That is a
judgement, not a test, and it is not enforceable as a condition of use anywhere the estate can
identify. It is published because it governs how the Steward will assess Tier 3 deployments, and
because §H.30's non-participation permission is meaningless without it.

## 5. What this table does not do

It does not predict outcomes in any jurisdiction, it does not constitute legal advice, and it
does not bind a court. Counsel review of these assertions is a
[launch blocker](../00-governance-map/launch-blockers.md#b-05--counsel-review-of-the-legal-instruments--hard-blocker),
and the jurisdiction and liability figures are unresolved at
[RAT-07](../00-governance-map/open-questions-and-ratifications.md#rat-07--jurisdiction-governing-law-and-the-liability-cap).

`[VERIFY]` The per-jurisdiction enforceability of the Track 2 representation claim is asserted
on general principle and has not been confirmed by counsel in any named forum. Tracked in
[Verification Log](../00-governance-map/verification-log.md).

**Two devices in §6 are untested legal instruments and the licence's own drafter says so.** The
condition/covenant split (§F.4) and the Scale Gate (§H.2-bis) have enforceability that *"varies
significantly between civil-law and common-law systems,"* and the text *"needs a lawyer's pass
before you publish it as binding"* — reproduced verbatim rather than paraphrased, and open at
[RAT-18](../00-governance-map/open-questions-and-ratifications.md#rat-18) with defect ML‑D‑010.
`counsel_reviewed: false` is in this file's front matter.

The distinction the estate must keep visible: **ML-2.3 governs MetaX's own conduct by the
Curator's adoption, and its enforceability against a third party is unreviewed.** No page in this
estate may render §6 as a prediction of any court's behaviour.
