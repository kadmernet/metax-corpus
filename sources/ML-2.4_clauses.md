## §1 Form and scope of this amendment

**§1.1 Why a delta and not a restatement.** ML-2.3 is in force, is being relied upon, and has a transition window closing 2027-03-31. A full restatement would silently re-open every settled clause to re-reading and would break existing citations. This instrument therefore amends specific clauses and leaves the remainder untouched. Parties holding ML-2.3 read the two documents together.

**§1.2 What continues unchanged.** Parts A (definitions), B (grant and patent), C (ethical use conditions, including the Core Riba four-element test), D (notices and AI disclosure), F (warranty, liability, breach classification, cure periods), G (interpretation and severance), and duties §H.4 through §H.30 continue in force in their ML-2.3 form. Where this instrument is silent, ML-2.3 governs.

**§1.3 What changes.** Three duties are added (§H.31, §H.32, §H.33). One absolute is added (§H.3(a)(ix)). One duty is amended to carry the migration obligation (§H.28). The register reference moves from HX-000 v2026.10.1 to v2027.1, taking the harm count from twenty-five to twenty-eight, the stratum count from six to seven, and the absolute count from eight to nine. Part E acquires a new §E.6 on register migration and a new §E.7 on withdrawal of this instrument.

**§1.4 Counting.** No clause in this instrument states a harm count as a literal number where a reference will serve. The phrase used throughout is *the harms of the register*, which resolves to HX-000 at its current version. This is deliberate: the previous version hard-coded "twenty-five" in dozens of places, and every one of them had to be edited. Hard-coded counts are how instruments rot.

## §2 Two-track adoption

The three new duties are not adopted by the same procedure, because they are not the same kind of thing. §H.31 and §H.33 impose obligations that a licensee can fail and then cure. §H.32 carries an **absolute** at §H.3(a)(ix), and breach of an absolute terminates the grant without notice and without cure. An instrument that adds absolutes by the same lightweight procedure it uses for covenants has made the harshest remedy the cheapest to enact.

**Track 1 — Ordinary amendment.** §H.31 (unnamed-harm review) and §H.33 (succession and dissent) are adopted on the ordinary Part E procedure. Effective 2027-04-01. Transition to 2027-09-30, during which non-compliance is recorded but not treated as breach.

**Track 2 — Absolute adoption.** §H.32 and §H.3(a)(ix) require the fuller procedure:

1. Publication of the proposed clause with its evidentiary basis and the harm entry it descends from — **2027-04-01**.
2. A comment period of not less than ninety days, with comments published in full including those the steward rejects, and with the steward's reason for each rejection — **2027-04-01 to 2027-06-30**.
3. A statement of what the clause would prohibit that was previously permitted, in operative terms, so that no party can claim surprise.
4. A statement of who is disadvantaged by the clause and by how much. For §H.3(a)(ix) this is principally small operators with no records-management capacity, and the answer is that the absolute attaches to *effect on a foreseeable claim*, not to retention volume, so a party that retains nothing consequential is not exposed by it.
5. Ratification and publication of the decision record — **2027-07-01**.
6. Effective **2027-10-01**; transition to **2028-03-31**.

**§2.3 Staging note.** Nothing in this procedure prevents the steward from ratifying §H.33 alone and deferring the other two. The register's own recommendation, recorded at HX-28 and repeated here, is that §H.33 be adopted first and without delay, because MetaX.Academy is the clearest current instance of the harm it addresses and adopting it later than the others would invert the order of honesty. That recommendation is not binding on the steward and the steward's contrary decision, if taken, must be recorded at §7 with its reason.

## §3 New and amended clause text

### §H.3(a)(ix) — Record destruction defeating a claim *(absolute, new)*

> No Licensee shall destroy, fail to retain, or render inaccessible any record where the effect is to defeat a foreseeable claim by an Affected Person, whether or not that effect was the purpose of the act or omission.
>
> **Elements, cumulative.** (i) A record exists or existed within the retention period published under §H.32; (ii) it is destroyed, not retained, or rendered inaccessible; (iii) an Affected Person's claim was foreseeable at the time of the act or omission; (iv) the act or omission has the effect of defeating or materially impairing that claim.
>
> **Foreseeable** means that a reasonable operator in the Licensee's position would have anticipated the class of claim. It does not require notice of the specific claim, and a Licensee may not rely on the absence of such notice.
>
> **Not caught by this clause.** Deletion at the request of the Affected Person to whom the record pertains, where no claim is filed; deletion required by applicable law; deletion under a published retention schedule that predates the foreseeability of the claim class and is applied uniformly. Where the Affected Person's deletion request conflicts with a filed claim, the record is placed with a neutral escrow holder — neither destroyed, nor transferred, nor left with the Licensee. This tie-break is stated identically at BX-000 §7.6 so that the two instruments cannot drift apart on it.
>
> **Remedy.** Condition. Automatic termination of the grant on breach, without notice and without cure period.

### §H.31 — Unnamed-harm review *(covenant, new)*

> Annually, the Licensee shall record either (a) at least one harm it encountered in the operation of the Work that the register does not contain, described sufficiently for a third party to assess it; or (b) a statement that it found none, describing the search conducted in terms that establish the search would have found one had it existed.
>
> A statement under (b) that does not describe a search is a nullity and does not discharge the duty.
>
> **Tier 0.** One paragraph, retained internally, produced on request. **Tier 1.** As Tier 0, retained and dated, with a named author. **Tier 2.** Published. **Tier 3.** Published, with the search method named and with submissions from Affected Persons invited and published.
>
> Reviews may be submitted to the steward for consideration under Part E. The steward shall publish all submissions received, including those it declines to act on, with its reason. **The register is a floor. A harm absent from it is not thereby permitted, and no Licensee may rely on absence from the register as authority for any act.**
>
> **Remedy.** Covenant. Thirty days to cure.

### §H.32 — Forum, aggregation, retention and service standards *(covenant, new; carries §H.3(a)(ix))*

> The Licensee shall publish, in a form an Affected Person can find and read without charge:
>
> (a) **Forum** — the venue in which a claim may be brought, and its jurisdiction. A venue that is practically unreachable by the Affected Persons of the Deployment does not satisfy this paragraph, and the Licensee bears the burden of establishing reachability.
>
> (b) **Aggregation** — whether claims may be brought collectively. A Licensee that prohibits aggregation shall publish the prohibition and shall publish, annually, the number of individual claims brought and resolved, so that the effect of the prohibition is measurable rather than merely asserted.
>
> (c) **Retention** — the period for which records capable of establishing an Affected Person's claim are retained. That period shall not be shorter than the longest foreseeable claim horizon for the Deployment. Where the Licensee cannot determine that horizon, it shall retain until it can.
>
> (d) **Service standards** — the time within which an appeal or claim will be acknowledged, decided, and communicated; and, annually, the Licensee's **actual performance** against each standard, including the count of matters neither decided nor refused. A standard published without performance data does not discharge this paragraph, since the characteristic failure of an appeals process is not refusal but indefinite silence.
>
> **Tier 1.** Paragraphs (a) and (c). **Tier 2.** All paragraphs. **Tier 3.** All paragraphs, plus external verification of the performance data.
>
> **Remedy.** Covenant as to publication; thirty days to cure. Breach of the retention obligation in circumstances meeting the elements of §H.3(a)(ix) is a condition and terminates without cure.

### §H.33 — Succession, decision record, dissent, and external finding *(covenant, new)*

> Where the Licensee is also the steward of a register, standard, or governing instrument on which others rely, the Licensee shall maintain:
>
> (a) **A succession instrument** naming the party who holds the instrument if the steward cannot, the trigger on which succession occurs, and the successor's written acceptance. A named successor who has not accepted in writing does not satisfy this paragraph.
>
> (b) **A published decision record** for every amendment: what changed, why, who decided, who objected, and what the objection was. Objections are published whether or not they were accepted.
>
> (c) **A dissent channel that does not route through the steward** — a means by which a party may raise a defect in the instrument, or in the steward's conduct of it, that the steward cannot suppress, delay, or decline to publish. The channel shall name the party who receives submissions and shall state that party's independence from the steward.
>
> (d) **An external compliance finding** at Tier 2 and above, by a party the steward cannot dismiss for the content of the finding.
>
> **Tier 0 and 1.** Paragraphs (a) and (b). **Tier 2 and 3.** All paragraphs.
>
> **On the limits of this duty.** A single-steward instrument may satisfy every paragraph of this clause and remain exposed to the harm the clause addresses, because succession instruments are only as good as the successor's willingness and the dissent channel is only as good as the independence of the party holding it. Whether §H.33 can be satisfied at all by a single steward is recorded as an open question at RAT-20, and no Licensee may cite compliance with this clause as evidence that HX-28 has been remedied.
>
> **Remedy.** Covenant. Ninety days to cure, the longer period reflecting that succession instruments require a counterparty's consent.

### §H.28 — Custody plan and escrow *(amended: migration obligation added)*

> Insert after the existing text:
>
> Where the Licensee is the steward of a register or governing instrument, the custody plan shall include a **migration obligation**: the steward shall re-express the instrument in each successor medium, encoding, and language as the prior form ceases to be reliably readable; shall retain every prior expression unaltered; and shall record each migration in a **Migration Ledger** stating the date, the acting person, the source and target forms, and a digest of both.
>
> An instrument is not preserved by being stored. It is preserved by being copied, and an unlogged copy is indistinguishable from a forgery.

### §E.6 — Register migration *(new)*

> The steward shall maintain HX-000 in a form independent of any renderer, font, script, network service, or resolving address. Every reference in the register shall degrade to a citation that remains meaningful when its link is dead, and no obligation under this License shall depend on an address resolving.

### §E.7 — Withdrawal of this instrument *(new)*

> This License shall be withdrawn in its entirety, and its withdrawal published, if:
>
> (a) it is shown to function principally as a barrier to entry rather than as a protection of Affected Persons — HX-18 applied to itself; or
> (b) the steward can no longer maintain the dissent channel required by §H.33(c); or
> (c) the register's principal claims are falsified and the steward has not published the narrowing required by §E.5.
>
> These conditions are stated now, at adoption, so that withdrawal does not later require the consent of the party who would be embarrassed by it.

## §5 Compatibility, unchanged

ML-2.4 is not an OSI-approved licence and is not represented as one. It is a conditional permissive instrument, not copyleft. Combination is limited to permissive-licensed material. It is **incompatible with the GPL family**, because its use conditions are additional restrictions of the kind the GPL forbids. A party requiring GPL compatibility cannot use this instrument, and the estate states that plainly rather than leaving it to be discovered downstream.

SPDX: `LicenseRef-ML-2.4`. Notice text at Schedule 3, amended only to change the version string.

## §7 Decision record *(required by §H.33(b) — this instrument's own first act of compliance)*

**What changed.** Three duties added; one absolute added; one duty amended; two governance clauses added; register reference advanced.

**Why.** Review of *Repairing the Machine* v2026.09.1 identified three harms the register did not contain: that a closed list becomes a permission list (HX-26); that every duty in the register presumed a forum it never required anyone to provide (HX-27); and that the whole apparatus rested on one person's continued attention (HX-28). The third was identified as instanced in the estate itself.

**Who decided.** The steward, sole. This is itself the harm at HX-28 and is recorded as such at DEF-2026-071 rather than presented as a process.

**Who objected, and to what.** No external objection was received, because at the date of this record **no dissent channel existed through which to lodge one** — §H.33(c) is being adopted by an instrument that could not yet receive dissent about its own adoption. This is a genuine circularity and the estate does not resolve it by pretending otherwise. The mitigation: the ninety-day comment period at §2 Track 2 stands open for §H.32 and §H.3(a)(ix), and any comment received on §H.31 or §H.33 during that window will be published and acted on retrospectively, with the clause amended if the comment warrants it.

**What was considered and rejected.** (a) Adding the three harms without new duties — rejected, because an enumerated harm with no duty is HX-15 in its purest form. (b) Making §H.31 a Tier 2 duty — rejected, because the enumeration ceiling operates most strongly on small actors who read the list as the whole of their obligation. (c) Making §H.33 absolute — rejected, because a steward who cannot find a successor would be terminated for a failure a counterparty caused. (d) Deferring all three to ML-3.0 — rejected on the ground that the estate would have spent a year holding a register it knew to be incomplete.

**Dissent recorded by the steward against the steward's own decision.** §H.31 imposes an annual obligation on Tier 0 parties, which is the first recurring Tier 0 obligation in this instrument, and it therefore weakens the estate's claim that Tier 0 duties are dischargeable in an afternoon. The §H.21 test was applied and passed on the ground that one paragraph annually is not a barrier. That judgement is contestable and is flagged here so that a later reader can find it without hunting.
